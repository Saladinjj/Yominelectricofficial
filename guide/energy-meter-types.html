Types of Energy Meters: Complete B2B Buyer Guide (2026)

Compare all types of energy meters — single phase, three phase, prepaid STS, smart WiFi, DIN rail, analog, and digital. Learn which meter type fits your application, what certifications matter, and how to source factory-direct from China.

Updated: July 2026 By Yomin Electric Engineering Team 10 min read

1. Single Phase Energy Meter

The single phase energy meter is the most widely used type — designed for residential and light commercial applications where the electrical supply is 220V-240V across two wires (line + neutral). These meters measure active energy consumption (kWh) and are available in both analog (electromechanical) and digital (electronic) versions.

Best for: Residential buildings, apartments, small shops, home sub-metering. Current range: 5(40)A to 10(100)A. IEC 62053-21 Class 1 or Class 2 accuracy.

Analog vs Digital Single Phase Meters

Analog meters use a rotating aluminum disc driven by electromagnetic induction. They are durable, inexpensive, and require no external power — but lack communication features. Digital meters use a microprocessor with LCD/LED display, offer pulse output, and can include RS485 MODBUS communication for remote reading.

2. Three Phase Energy Meter

Three phase energy meters are designed for commercial buildings, industrial facilities, and utility distribution networks operating on 3-phase, 4-wire (3P4W) or 3-phase, 3-wire (3P3W) systems at 230/400V. They measure active, reactive, and apparent power across all three phases.

Best for: Factories, commercial buildings, EV charging stations, solar PV monitoring, utility grid metering. Current: direct up to 100A or CT-operated for 100A+. Accuracy: Class 0.5S, 1, or 2.

Direct-Connect vs CT-Operated

Direct-connect meters (up to 100A) wire the load current directly through the meter. CT-operated meters use external current transformers for loads above 100A — common in industrial panels and substations where the primary current may be 200A, 400A, or higher. CT-operated meters offer greater flexibility and are the standard for revenue-grade utility metering.

3. Prepaid STS Keypad Energy Meter

Prepaid STS meters use the Standard Transfer Specification (STS) — an internationally recognized protocol for electricity token generation. Users purchase a 20-digit token (via mobile money, bank, or vendor), enter it on the meter keypad, and the meter credits the corresponding kWh. When credit runs out, the meter disconnects via an internal relay.

Best for: Utilities and municipalities in Africa, Middle East, South Asia. Eliminates billing collection costs, reduces revenue loss from non-payment. Compliant with STS 531-1/2/3.

Key features: Built-in contactor/relay for load disconnect, tamper detection, low-credit alarm, emergency credit buffer, and support for multiple tariff rates. Our prepaid meters are deployed in Kenya, Nigeria, Ghana, Tanzania, Nepal, and Pakistan with full local certification.

4. Smart WiFi / IoT Energy Meter

Smart energy meters connect to the internet via WiFi, 4G, or LoRaWAN — sending real-time consumption data to a cloud platform or mobile app. These are the fastest-growing segment, driven by utility digitalization and smart home adoption.

Unlike basic digital meters, smart meters support: remote disconnect/reconnect, time-of-use (TOU) tariffs, load profiling, power quality monitoring (voltage, current, PF, frequency), and over-the-air (OTA) firmware updates. Common protocols: MODBUS TCP/RTU, DL/T645, MQTT.

Best for: Smart buildings, EV charging, solar + storage systems, energy service companies (ESCOs), utility AMI projects. Communication: WiFi, 4G/GPRS, Zigbee, LoRaWAN.

5. DIN Rail Mounted Energy Meter

DIN rail energy meters are compact (1P to 7P modules wide) and snap onto standard 35mm DIN rails inside distribution panels. They are the go-to format for sub-metering in multi-tenant buildings, office fit-outs, and industrial control panels.

Available in single phase (2P-4P) and three phase (7P) configurations with optional RS485, pulse output, and backlit LCD. Most models are MID certified (EU Measuring Instruments Directive) for billing applications in Europe.

When sourcing energy meters from China, confirm your supplier can provide the right certifications for your target market:

  • CE (EU) — European conformity, required for EU + many African markets
  • MID 2014/32/EU — Legal metrology for billing in EU countries
  • STS 531 — Prepaid token interoperability standard
  • ISO 9001 — Factory quality management system
  • RoHS — Restriction of hazardous substances
  • EN 50470 — European standard for active energy meters

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between Class 1 and Class 0.5S energy meters?

Class 1 meters have ±1% accuracy across the full load range and are standard for residential and commercial billing. Class 0.5S meters offer ±0.5% accuracy and are required for revenue-grade utility metering and large industrial consumers. The "S" designation means the meter maintains accuracy even at very low loads (down to 1% of rated current).

Can I mix different meter types in one building?

Yes — many multi-tenant commercial buildings use a combination: a CT-operated three-phase meter at the main incomer for utility billing, then DIN rail single-phase meters for individual tenant sub-metering. All meters can feed into a central RS485 or WiFi energy management system.
