How to Choose a Current Transformer: Ratio, Class and Burden

ET
ET Engineering Dept 7 min read August 2, 2026

Current transformers (CTs) scale high currents down to safe, measurable levels. Choosing the wrong one means inaccurate billing, nuisance trips, or protection that fails to operate.

Ratio: Match the Primary Current

The ratio (e.g., 200/5 A) tells you the primary current per 5 A at the secondary. Pick a ratio where the load's normal operating current sits at 50–80% of the CT rating — oversized CTs lose accuracy at light load, undersized ones saturate.

Accuracy Class: Metering vs Protection

Metering CTs use classes like 0.5 and 1.0 for billing-grade accuracy across the normal range. Protection CTs (5P10, 10P10) prioritize fidelity during faults. Know which duty your CT serves — never substitute one for the other.

Burden: The Load on the Secondary

Burden (VA) is the load the secondary winding drives: wires, meters and relays. Exceeding the rated burden degrades accuracy; undersizing the wires adds error. Sum all connected devices and add margin.

Window and Installation

Split-core CTs wrap around existing cables without disconnecting them — perfect for retrofits. Solid-core CTs must be threaded onto the conductor during installation. Confirm the window diameter fits your cable or busbar.

Conclusion

Ratio, class and burden are the three numbers that matter. Match them to your load profile and accuracy requirements, and your metering will be right the first time.
