What Is a Current Transformer Meter

ET
ET Engineering Team 8 min read August 20, 2026

A current transformer meter is an energy metering setup that uses a current transformer (CT) to measure electricity in high-current circuits. Instead of passing the full supply current through the meter, the CT steps it down to a safe, standard value — typically 5 A or 1 A — that the meter can read accurately. This makes CT metering the standard approach for industrial, commercial, and utility installations.

How a current transformer meter works

The CT is clamped around or wired into the main conductor. It produces a secondary current proportional to the primary current, and the meter multiplies this by the CT ratio to display the real energy consumption in kWh. For example, a 400/5 A CT with a meter reading 2.5 A secondary means the primary current is 200 A.

CT ratio: the key number

The ratio (e.g., 100/5, 400/5, 1000/5) must match the expected load. Choose a primary rating at about 60-80% of the maximum load for best metering accuracy — too high loses low-load accuracy, too low risks saturation.

Solid-core, split-core, or Rogowski?

  • Solid-core CT — highest accuracy; must be installed with the conductor disconnected.
  • Split-core CT — clamps around a live cable without disconnection; ideal for retrofits.
  • Rogowski coil — flexible, non-saturating, for large or irregular conductors and wide measurement ranges.

Where CT meters are used

  • Main incoming feeders in commercial and industrial switchboards.
  • Submetering of tenants, floors, or departments.
  • Solar and EV infrastructure monitoring.
  • Utility metering and power distribution.

Safety rules

The secondary of a current transformer must never be opened while primary current flows — it can generate dangerous voltages. Terminals must be shorted before disconnection and the CT grounded as specified.

FAQ

When do I need a CT meter instead of a direct meter?

When the load exceeds about 100 A, or when the busbar/cable is too large for direct connection.

Can I add a CT to an existing meter?

Only if the meter is designed for CT operation — direct-connect meters cannot use CTs.

Is a Rogowski coil a current transformer?

It is a related sensing technology with no iron core: linear, non-saturating, and flexible, but its output needs an integrator.
