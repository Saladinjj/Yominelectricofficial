Waterproof Junction Box Guide: IP Ratings and Sealed Cable Joints

ET
ET Engineering Team September 17, 2026 8 min read

A junction box either keeps water out for the life of the installation or it does not. There is no middle ground and no way to inspect it later, because the joint inside is sealed by definition. That makes the specification decision permanent, and it makes the two numbers that matter worth understanding properly.

What an IP rating actually promises

The IP code has two digits and they are not interchangeable. The first is solids, the second is liquids. A box rated IP67 is dust-tight and protected against temporary immersion. A box rated IP65 is dust-tight and protected against water jets, but is not rated for immersion.

RatingSolidsLiquidsWhere it belongs
IP54Dust protectedSplashing waterSheltered indoor plant rooms
IP65Dust tightWater jetsOutdoor under eaves, washdown areas
IP66Dust tightPowerful water jetsExposed outdoor, marine decks
IP67Dust tightTemporary immersionGround level, pits, flood-prone sites

The common error is treating IP65 and IP67 as a quality ladder when they are different tests. An IP65 box mounted at ground level where water pools will fail, and no amount of thicker plastic changes that.

The gasket is the seal, not the enclosure

The plastic or metal body of a junction box is mostly a container. The water resistance comes from the compressible gasket that sits in a channel around the rim, closing the gap between lid and base when the corner screws are tightened.

That has three practical consequences. The gasket must be compressed evenly, which means tightening the corner screws in a diagonal sequence rather than running round the box. It must not be damaged or contaminated during assembly. And it has a working temperature range, so a gasket chosen for a cold store and one chosen for a roof in direct sun are not the same part.

Because the gasket is hidden once the lid is on, buy from a supplier who can tell you the material rather than just the rating.

Glands decide whether the rating survives

A perfectly sealed box with an undersized or unmated gland is not sealed. Cable entries are where most IP failures actually occur.

  • Match the gland to the cable diameter, not to the cable's nominal size. Gripping range, not conductor area, is the number that determines whether the seal closes.
  • Use the correct thread. A gland forced into a mismatched thread will not seat squarely and will leak at the shoulder.
  • Seal every unused entry. An open knockout is a direct path to the interior regardless of the lid gasket.
  • Do not over-tighten. Excess torque distorts the gland body and unseats the seal it was meant to compress.

Choosing the enclosure itself

  • Material — ABS for general indoor and sheltered use, polycarbonate where impact or UV exposure matters, metal where mechanical protection or screening is required.
  • Internal volume — the box must hold the joint plus the bend radius of the cables. A box filled solid cannot be closed without trapping conductors against the gasket.
  • Mounting — wall, panel or pole, and whether the fixing holes are outside the sealed area. Fixings through the interior compromise the seal.
  • Customisation — if the entry positions or internal mounting posts need to change, confirm this at order stage. Post-moulding drilling voids most IP claims.

Installation practice that protects the rating

Drill and deburr all entries before mounting the box, never in place above the joint. Use the gasket dry and clean; sealant is a substitute for a correct gasket, not an upgrade. Fit the lid with all its screws, because an IP rating assumes the enclosure is complete. And where the box will be immersed, mount it so water cannot stand against the gasket line.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between IP65 and IP67?

IP65 protects against dust and water jets but not immersion. IP67 protects against dust and temporary immersion. They are different tests, not points on a quality scale, so an IP65 box at ground level where water pools will fail.

What makes a junction box waterproof, the enclosure or the gasket?

The gasket. The enclosure is a container; water resistance comes from the compressible gasket in the rim channel, closed by tightening the corner screws evenly. The gasket material and its condition decide whether the rating holds.

Why do waterproof boxes still leak?

Almost always at the cable entries rather than the lid. Glands matched to the wrong cable diameter, forced into the wrong thread, over-tightened, or unused knockouts left open all bypass the gasket seal entirely.
