How to Read Your Electric Meter and Measure Usage

ET
ET Engineering Team 7 min read August 20, 2026

Knowing how to read your electric meter and measure usage is one of the simplest ways to understand your electricity bill and find waste. Whether your meter is digital, electromechanical, or smart, the reading technique is easy to learn — and the same math applies everywhere.

How to read a digital electric meter

Digital meters show the reading on an LCD. Read the digits from left to right and ignore any that are red, in a red frame, or after a decimal point. Some digital meters cycle through screens — wait for the display showing total kWh (often labelled kWh or IMP) before noting the value.

How to read a dial (electromechanical) meter

Dial meters have five or six dials with alternating directions. Read each dial from left to right, noting the last number the needle has passed. If a needle sits directly on a number, check the dial to the right: if that right dial has not passed zero, use the lower number on the left dial.

How to calculate your usage

To measure usage between two dates, subtract the previous reading from the current one:

Current reading − previous reading = kWh used in the period

Example: today’s reading 12,810 kWh − last month’s 12,450 kWh = 360 kWh used. Multiply by your tariff to estimate the cost.

How to track usage like a pro

  • Read at the same time each week and log the numbers.
  • Compare week-over-week to catch sudden jumps.
  • Watch the overnight baseline — a high one means standby waste.
  • Switch one appliance off at a time to identify heavy users.
  • Use a smart meter with app or RS485 monitoring to automate tracking.

Common reading mistakes

  • Including decimal or red-framed digits.
  • Copying the wrong register on multi-tariff meters.
  • Forgetting the multiplier when a meter is CT-operated.
  • Comparing readings from different dates.

FAQ

Which digits do I read on my meter?

All whole-number digits from left to right; ignore red-framed and decimal digits.

How often should I read my meter?

Weekly is ideal for tracking; monthly is enough for bill verification.

Do smart meters need manual reading?

No — they send readings automatically, but you can still read the display yourself.

Final Thoughts

Reading your electric meter takes seconds, but the data it provides can save real money all year. Start with a simple log, compare periods, and let the numbers guide your energy habits — supported by a reliable energy meter from Yomin Electric.
