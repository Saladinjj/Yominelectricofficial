Control Panels & Power Distribution Blocks / Terminal Blocks & Connectors

Control Panels: What Is a Power Distribution Block?

ET
ET Engineering Team October 22, 2026 10 min read

Streamlining Power Distribution Inside Industrial Electrical Enclosures

Inside electrical control panels, switchgear cubicles, and machinery power distribution units, power distribution is an everyday engineering requirement: a primary electrical supply (for example, a 160A or 250A feeder cable entering from the main disconnect switch) must be subdivided into branch circuits feeding multiple variable frequency drives (VFDs), motor starters, miniature circuit breakers, and auxiliary power supplies.

Historically, panel builders relied on custom copper busbar fabrications or hazardous terminal jumper bars. Today, the modular DIN rail power distribution block (UKK Series) has become the international industry standard for clean, safe, and code-compliant power distribution.

Engineering Advantages of Modular Unipolar Distribution Blocks

  • High Current Carrying Efficiency: The solid tinned brass conductor block provides an exceptionally low-resistance current path, ensuring minimal voltage drop and low operational temperature rise during continuous full-load operation.
  • Universal Conductor Compatibility: Each tunnel terminal accommodates solid, stranded, or fine-stranded flexible copper conductors fitted with bootlace ferrules. Precision hex-socket set screws deliver uniform clamping pressure without severing fragile conductor strands.
  • Modular Gangability: UKK blocks can be mounted individually or interlocked side-by-side on a standard 35mm DIN rail to create multi-phase power distribution assemblies (3-Phase 3-Wire, 3-Phase 4-Wire, or DC positive/negative pairs) with minimal footprint.
  • High Short-Circuit Withstand Capability (SCCR): High mechanical strength PA66 housing and robust internal geometry prevent catastrophic terminal rupture during downstream short-circuit faults.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a power distribution block and why is it used instead of daisy-chain terminal wiring?

A power distribution block (PDB, often termed a unipolar distribution block or modular feeder splitter) is an engineered electrical terminal device designed to receive a single large incoming power feeder conductor and split it into multiple smaller outgoing branch circuit conductors. <strong>Why daisy-chaining terminals is dangerous:</strong> <ul><li><strong>Thermal Overloading:</strong> Jamming two or three wires into a single circuit breaker or standard terminal clamp creates uneven mechanical pressure, leading to loose strands, localized hotspots, and electrical arcing.</li><li><strong>Code Violations:</strong> Most electrical installation codes (such as NEC Article 110.14 and IEC 61439-1) strictly prohibit terminating multiple conductors into a terminal lug unless specifically tested and rated for multi-wire use.</li><li><strong>Space and Aesthetics:</strong> Daisy-chaining creates a chaotic "bird's nest" of jumpers inside the wire ducting, impeding airflow and complicating troubleshooting.</li></ul>A dedicated distribution block provides a compact, centralized, and certified splitting point with zero wire crowding.

How does a power distribution block achieve IP20 finger-safe protection?

Under IEC 60529, an IP20 rating certifies that live hazardous electrical parts cannot be touched by a standardized test finger ($12.5\, ext{mm}$ diameter probe). YOMIN's UKK series power distribution blocks feature a fully enclosed insulating polyamide body and a transparent polycarbonate hinged top cover. All screw access ports and wire entry funnels are deeply recessed. Panel assembly technicians and maintenance engineers can inspect wire terminations and torque screws safely without accidental contact with live 1000V electrical conductors.

Can power distribution blocks be used for DC voltages in solar photovoltaic systems and energy storage (BESS)?

Yes. High-quality power distribution blocks manufactured with tinned brass conductors and UL 94V-0 polyamide housings are rated for both $690\, ext{V AC}$ and $1000\, ext{V DC}$. In solar combiner boxes and battery energy storage system (BESS) racks, UKK distribution blocks are widely used to parallel multiple positive or negative DC string inputs before feeding the main DC isolation switch or inverter input, providing robust, corrosion-resistant current sharing.
