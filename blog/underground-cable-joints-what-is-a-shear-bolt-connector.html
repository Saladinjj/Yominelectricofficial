Underground Cable Joints & Splices: What Is a Shear Bolt Connector?

ET
ET Engineering Team October 10, 2026 10 min read

The Mechanical Evolution in Underground Power Cable Jointing

Across modern electrical power distribution networks, renewable wind and solar farms, and urban grid infrastructure, high-voltage underground cabling operating at 1kV, 11kV, 24kV, and 33kV routes immense blocks of electrical power beneath city streets and industrial parks.

When connecting, splicing, or terminating these heavy cross-linked polyethylene (XLPE) insulated power cables, jointers must create conductor connections capable of carrying continuous loads of hundreds of amperes and withstanding violent short-circuit fault surges exceeding 25,000 Amperes.

For decades, utilities relied on hydraulic compression crimp sleeves and lugs. However, compression crimping demands heavy hydraulic presses, dozens of expensive matching dies, and rigorous training. A single jointer mistake—such as selecting an incorrect die size, incomplete pump strokes, or misaligned crimps—creates high-resistance joints that overheat and cause catastrophic cable joint explosions beneath city streets.

The Torque Shear Bolt Mechanical Cable Connector (Model BLMT / GRL Series) represents the definitive modern standard: precision-machined, electro-tinned aluminium alloy connectors equipped with factory-calibrated torque shear bolts that eliminate hydraulic tools entirely and guarantee mathematically perfect contact pressure on every installation.

Engineering Anatomy: Inside a Certified Shear Bolt Mechanical Cable Connector

  1. High-Tensile Tinned Aluminium Alloy Body: Forged from premium electrical-grade aluminium alloy and heavily electro-tinned to ensure zero galvanic corrosion when joining copper to aluminium conductors.
  2. Factory-Calibrated Torque Shear Bolts: Precision-machined friction bolts with engineered torsional fracture planes that shear off cleanly at the exact required clamping torque (25–35 Nm).
  3. Flush-Break Multi-Stepped Bolt Design: The bolt head snaps off flush with the outer connector barrel, leaving a smooth cylindrical profile that will not puncture cold-shrink or heat-shrink silicone joint tubes.
  4. Internally Grooved Grooves with Pre-Filled Antioxidant Paste: Sharp circumferential inner grooves bite through tough non-conductive aluminium oxide ($Al_2O_3$) surface films, while factory-filled synthetic grease seals out oxygen and moisture.
  5. Centering Reduction Sleeves (Bushings): Removable aluminium centering inserts allow a single connector body to clamp small conductor cross-sections (e.g., 25 mm²) with perfectly centered alignment alongside large 95 mm² or 240 mm² cables.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a Shear Bolt Connector and why is it replacing traditional hydraulic compression crimp lugs?

A Shear Bolt Connector—frequently designated as a mechanical bolted connector, torque shear lug, or range-taking bolted sleeve—is a specialized electrical cable jointing and termination connector engineered from high-strength tinned aluminium alloy. Instead of relying on hydraulic compression tools, it uses precision-machined contact bolts with engineered shear-off heads. It is rapidly replacing traditional compression crimp lugs across utility underground distribution networks for four decisive engineering reasons: <ul><li><strong>1. Complete Elimination of Hydraulic Crimping Tools &amp; Dies:</strong> Traditional compression joints require heavy hydraulic crimping pumps and dozens of specialized hexagonal dies tailored to every cable size and standard (DIN, metric, AWG). In cramped underground trenches or aloft in buckets, carrying heavy pumps is arduous. A shear bolt connector installs using nothing more than a standard cordless impact driver or ratchet socket wrench.</li><li><strong>2. Guaranteed Calibrated Contact Pressure (Zero Human Error):</strong> With manual crimping, under-crimping creates high-resistance hotspots that burn out under load, while over-crimping severs conductor strands. Shear bolts feature factory-calibrated torsional weak points. The bolt tightens into the conductor until the exact required contact pressure (e.g., 25–35 Nm) is achieved, at which point the driving head <strong>automatically snaps off flush</strong>, eliminating operator guesswork and guaranteeing a mathematically perfect connection every time.</li><li><strong>3. Wide Range-Taking Versatility (Inventory Reduction):</strong> While a compression lug fits exactly ONE cable cross-section, a single shear bolt connector accommodates four to five different conductor sizes (e.g., 95 mm&sup2;, 120 mm&sup2;, 150 mm&sup2;, 185 mm&sup2;, and 240 mm&sup2;). This slashes utility emergency stockkeeping inventory by up to 75%.</li><li><strong>4. Universal Bi-Metallic Compatibility:</strong> Precision tin-plating and specialized contact grease allow the same connector to join aluminium to aluminium, copper to copper, or transition aluminium to copper without galvanic corrosion or unequal thermal expansion failure.</li></ul>

How does the shear-off bolt achieve a smooth, flush surface suitable for cold-shrink joint sleeves?

In medium-voltage cable joints (11kV to 36kV), any sharp metallic protrusion creates extreme electrical field concentrations that puncture insulation sleeves. Older bolted connectors left jagged bolt stubs that required tedious manual filing. YOMIN BLMT Shear Bolt Connectors utilize advanced <strong>multi-stepped threaded shear bolts</strong>. As the bolt torques down on smaller conductors, deeper thread steps engage; on larger conductors, outer steps engage. The bolt always fractures cleanly at or slightly below the outer cylindrical surface of the connector barrel. The resulting joint presents a smooth, rounded metallic profile that slides directly into cold-shrink or heat-shrink insulating sleeves without filling putty or filing.

What is the significance of IEC 61238-1 Class A certification for underground shear bolt connectors?

IEC 61238-1-3 Class A is the most rigorous international performance benchmark for power cable connectors. To achieve Class A certification, the connector must endure: <ul><li><strong>1000 Electrical Heat Cycles:</strong> Rapid heating to 120&deg;C via heavy current injection followed by forced cooling, proving contact resistance stays flat.</li><li><strong>Short-Circuit Current Withstand Tests:</strong> Surviving multiple short-circuit fault surges up to 25kA / 1 second without mechanical loosening or thermal runaway.</li><li><strong>Tensile Pull-Out Strength Tests:</strong> Demonstrating that conductor grip easily exceeds utility mechanical tension thresholds.</li></ul>
