IP44 vs IP66 Industrial Plugs and Sockets: Which Rating to Specify

ET
ET Engineering Team October 28, 2026 9 min read

IP44 protects against splashing water and solid objects larger than 1 mm. IP66 protects against dust ingress entirely and against powerful water jets from any direction. The practical difference is not a small refinement: IP44 suits a socket under a canopy or on a scaffold, IP66 suits a washdown bay or a plant full of conductive dust. Neither number describes the plug on its own — both describe the plug and socket mated together, lid closed, with a cable gland matched to the cable.

The two digits, decoded

The code comes from IEC 60529. The first digit is solids, the second is water, and each digit answers a different question from the one buyers usually ask.

DigitFirst digit — solidsSecond digit — water
4Protected against solid objects 1 mm and largerProtected against splashing water from any direction
5Dust protected: some dust may enter but not in a quantity that interferes with operationProtected against water jets (6.3 mm nozzle) from any direction
6Dust tight: no dust ingress at allProtected against powerful water jets (12.5 mm nozzle) from any direction
6K—Increased pressure jet, for high-pressure cleaning at closer range
7—Protected against temporary immersion (30 minutes at a stated depth)
8—Protected against continuous immersion under stated conditions

An X in place of a digit means that protection was not tested, not that it is absent. IPX4 is a real declaration: splash protection was verified, solids were not part of the test.

IP44: the working default for outdoor power

Most IEC 60309 industrial plugs and sockets are built to IP44, and for good reason. A 16 A or 32 A connector with a spring-loaded lid keeps rain out of a socket on a scaffold, resists hose spray from a distance, and tolerates workshop dust. Blue bodies mark single-phase 230 V, red bodies mark three-phase 400 V, and the clock position of the earth pin fixes the voltage and frequency so that a wrong plug will not enter a socket.

IP44 is enough for temporary site distribution, workshops, canopies, marquees and outdoor sockets under cover. It is not enough for a food production floor, a concrete batching plant, or any location where a pressure washer is aimed at the socket at close range.

IP66: dust-tight and jet-proof

Here the first 6 matters as much as the second. Dust-tight means no ingress at all, which is what grain, cement, carbon black and metal powder demand: conductive dust that reaches a terminal does not simply make the enclosure dirty, it creates a tracking path. The second 6 adds resistance to powerful jets, which is the washdown case.

IP66 is not IP67. A washdown hose is a jet. Water standing around the base of a socket is immersion, and the correct rating for that is IP67 or IP68 depending on depth and duration. Buying IP66 for a location that floods is a common and expensive mis-specification.

Why the mated pair decides the rating

A plug rated IP66 inserted into a socket rated IP44 produces an IP44 connection. The system rating is the lower of the two components, and the same logic applies to every other part of the assembly:

  • The lid. The rating assumes the lid is closed and its seal is intact. A lid propped open by a cable is an open enclosure.
  • The gland. The cable entry must match the cable diameter. A gland tightened onto an undersized cable leaves a path that no plug rating can compensate for.
  • The mating face. Sealing rings on the plug shroud are what exclude water at the joint; a worn or missing ring drops the whole assembly to whatever the housing alone provides.
  • The mounting. A socket mounted upside down, or with the cable entry facing upward, collects water against the seal instead of draining away from it.

Where the cost actually sits

An IP number is cheap to print on a label and expensive to verify. Three questions separate a real declaration from a marketing one: which standard the rating is declared to, whether the figure applies to the component or to the mated assembly, and what the cable range of the gland is. A fourth matters in hot or sunny locations: the housing material. Flame-retardant polycarbonate and ABS behave differently under ultraviolet light and impact, and a cracked lid has no rating at all.

Checklist before you order

  • The rating you actually need, and the standard it must be declared to.
  • The mated pair: plug and socket specified together, not separately.
  • Voltage, frequency and phase count at the point of use.
  • Current rating, and whether the load is continuous or intermittent.
  • Pin configuration: 3P+E or 3P+N+E, and the clock position that matches your supply.
  • Cable diameter against the gland range, including the largest cable you will ever fit.
  • Environment: ultraviolet, impact, chemicals, ambient temperature, and whether water pools at the base.
  • Upstream protection: RCD and breaker ratings, and who tests them after installation.

Frequently asked questions

Is IP66 better than IP44?

It is more protective and more expensive. For a socket under a canopy, IP44 does the job. IP66 earns its cost where dust is conductive or where a hose is used at close range; paying for it on a sheltered workshop wall buys nothing.

Can I use an IP66 plug in an IP44 socket?

It will fit if the interface is the same, but the connection is rated IP44 — the lower of the two. The seal is made at the mating face, and one weak side is enough to let water in.

What does IPX4 mean?

That splash protection was tested and the solids digit was not. It is not a claim of no dust protection, only a statement that dust was outside the scope of the test.

Does IP66 mean waterproof?

No. IP66 covers powerful jets, not immersion. If water can stand around the enclosure, specify IP67, and for continuous immersion IP68.

Does the lid have to be closed to keep the rating?

Yes. The rating applies to the enclosure as tested, with the lid closed and its gasket seated. A socket in use with the lid open is protected only by the plug interface.

Specifying power for an outdoor or washdown area?

Tell us the rating, voltage, current and cable size, and where the socket will be installed. We will confirm the configuration before anything is quoted.
