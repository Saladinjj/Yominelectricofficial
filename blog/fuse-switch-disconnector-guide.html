Fuse Switch Disconnector: What It Is, Ratings & How to Choose

ET Engineering Team 6 min read · September 1, 2026

Direct answer: a fuse switch disconnector is a manual switching device that combines two jobs in one unit: a fuse that interrupts short circuits and overloads, and a disconnector that gives you a visible, safe isolation point for maintenance. You size it by rated current, breaking capacity and the system it protects.

Panel builders and electrical distributors deal with these on every project — they're the standard way to protect and isolate LV feeders, distribution boards and increasingly the DC side of PV plants. Understanding the ratings makes the difference between a compliant panel and a callback.

Fuse Switch vs Disconnector vs Circuit Breaker

DeviceOverload/short-circuit protectionVisible isolationTypical use
Fuse switch disconnectorYes (fuse element)Yes (switching mechanism)LV feeders, distribution boards, PV DC
Isolating switchNoYesMaintenance isolation only
MCCB / breakerYes (thermal-magnetic)PartialWhere reclosing is needed

Key Ratings

  • Rated current (A): 80 A to 630 A common for LV switch disconnectors; match the feeder load.
  • Breaking capacity: the fault current the unit can clear — HV/LV ratings matter for fault-level coordination.
  • Fuse type: NH (blade) fuses for distribution; PV DC fuses (e.g. gPV) for solar strings.
  • Poles: 1P to 4P depending on single- or three-phase.
  • Enclosure / degree of protection: IP ratings for outdoor or dusty environments.

Selection Checklist

  1. What's the load current? → pick the nearest standard rating above it
  2. AC or DC side? → PV systems need DC-rated fuse switch disconnectors (gPV fuses)
  3. Fault level on site? → confirm the breaking capacity matches your coordination study
  4. How many poles? → single-phase 1P/2P, three-phase 3P/4P
  5. Indoor or outdoor? → check the IP rating

FAQ

Can I use a fuse switch disconnector as the main isolator?

Yes — that's one of its two core jobs. The switching mechanism provides visible isolation for safe maintenance.

Do PV systems need special fuse switches?

Yes. DC arc behavior is different from AC, so use DC-rated units with gPV fuses. Our NH1 PV DC solar fuses and DC isolators cover that side.
