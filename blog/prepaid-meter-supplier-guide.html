Prepaid Meter Supplier Guide: What Importers and Utilities Should Look For

ET
ET Engineering Team 9 min read August 28, 2026

A prepaid meter is an electricity meter that requires payment before consumption: the customer buys credit (a token, card or remote top-up), and the meter disconnects when credit runs out. For utilities, prepaid metering solves revenue collection, reduces theft and improves cash flow. For distributors and importers, it is one of the highest-demand metering segments — particularly in South Asia, Africa and Southeast Asia.

Choosing the right prepaid meter supplier means matching the payment technology to your market, verifying tamper protection and standards compliance, and working with a manufacturer that can support utility-scale rollouts. This guide covers all three.

How Prepaid Metering Works

Prepaid meters combine three functions: measurement (kWh counting), credit management (the meter's "wallet"), and disconnection (a relay that cuts supply when credit hits zero). The buyer-facing part is the recharge channel, and that is where technologies differ:

TechnologyHow the customer rechargesBest for
STS keypad (token)Customer buys a 20-digit token from a vending system and types it into the meter keypadUtility rollouts; interoperable, vendor-neutral standard (IEC 62055-41)
IC / smart cardCustomer loads credit onto a card at a vending point, inserts it in the meterMarkets where card networks already exist; offline operation
GPRS / remoteCredit is delivered over the network; meter reports usage remotelyUtility monitoring, tamper alarms, prepaid + data collection combined

For background, our what is a prepaid meter guide explains the concept in depth, and prepaid vs postpaid compares the two billing models.

Choosing the Right Technology for Your Market

  • STS (Standard Transfer Specification) is the safest choice for utility programs: it is an open international standard (IEC 62055-41), which means tokens work across different manufacturers' meters. If your market already runs STS vending, buy STS meters.
  • IC-card prepaid suits distributor-led programs and markets with established card vending. Simpler infrastructure, fully offline.
  • GPRS/remote prepaid fits utilities that want prepaid plus remote disconnect/reconnect, load limiting and data for grid analytics — at higher hardware and connectivity cost.

Many suppliers, including Yomin Electric, offer all three — the decision should be driven by your market's infrastructure, not by what a supplier happens to stock.

What Utilities and Distributors Look For in a Prepaid Meter

  1. Tamper protection — anti-tamper features are the core value of prepaid metering: detection of terminal cover opening, magnetic interference, bypass and neutral removal, with event logging and alarms.
  2. Standards compliance — IEC 62053-21 accuracy, IEC 62055-41 for STS token meters, IEC 62052-11 general requirements, plus CE where required.
  3. Disconnection reliability — the internal relay must handle repeated switching (disconnect/reconnect cycles) without failure; ask for the relay rating and cycle life.
  4. Billing integration — vending software, token generation, reports and integration with existing billing systems.
  5. Audit and event logs — recorded tamper events, power interruptions and credit transactions for dispute resolution.
  6. Low-power and accuracy stability — stable accuracy across the load range, including light loads typical of households.

Tamper Protection: The Real Value of Prepaid

Prepaid metering's business case rests on stopping revenue leakage, so tamper detection quality separates good meters from bad ones. Ask the supplier for the specific tamper events their meter detects:

  • Terminal cover opening detection
  • Magnetic field interference detection
  • Current bypass / shunt detection
  • Neutral disconnection detection
  • Event logging with date/time stamps and tamper alarms

A supplier that lists concrete detection mechanisms is more credible than one that just says "anti-tamper".

Buying Prepaid Meters for Import: Supplier Checklist

  1. Confirm the vending standard: STS (IEC 62055-41), IC card, or GPRS — and that the meter interoperates with your existing or planned vending system.
  2. Verify certifications: IEC 62053-21, CE; utility type approval where your market requires it.
  3. Request a sample and test: token entry, disconnection at zero credit, tamper events, accuracy at low load.
  4. Ask about customization: tariff display, language, logo, label and packaging for your market.
  5. Check batch testing and the defective-rate commitment before shipment.
  6. Confirm spare relay, display and communication module availability for field maintenance.
  7. Verify the supplier's export experience in your region — ask for references from utilities or distributors nearby.

FAQ

What is the difference between STS and IC-card prepaid meters?

STS meters accept 20-digit tokens generated by a vending system; tokens are vendor-neutral per IEC 62055-41, so any STS meter reads any STS token. IC-card meters store credit on a physical card that must be loaded at a vending point and inserted in the meter.

Do prepaid meters work without internet?

Yes. STS and IC-card meters work fully offline — credit is transferred via token or card. GPRS meters need a network connection but add remote management and tamper alarms.

Can a prepaid meter be converted to postpaid?

Many prepaid meters support both modes via configuration, but the tariff, vending and billing workflows differ. Confirm the meter's dual-mode capability with the supplier if you may switch later.

What accuracy class do prepaid meters use?

Class 1.0 is standard for residential prepaid metering under IEC 62053-21. Utility specifications sometimes require Class 1.0 with tighter low-load performance — confirm the accuracy requirements of your utility customer.
