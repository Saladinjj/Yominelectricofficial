Voltage Stabilizer Sizing: Matching the Right kVA to Your Load

ET
ET Engineering Team 6 min read July 25, 2026

A voltage stabilizer protects equipment from grid fluctuations — but only if it is sized correctly. The two classic mistakes are buying too small (nuisance trips) or too large (wasted money and poor regulation at light load).

Calculate Total Load Power

Add the power of every device the stabilizer will feed. For motors, compressors and pumps, apply a starting surge factor of 3–5× rated power — this is where undersized units fail.

Add a Safety Margin

Choose a stabilizer rated 20–30% above the calculated maximum. This covers future additions and keeps the regulator operating in its most efficient band rather than at its limit.

Single vs Three Phase

Confirm your supply is single-phase or three-phase and buy the matching unit. Three-phase stabilizers must be sized by the highest-loaded phase, not the average.

Check Input Range and Output

Verify the stabilizer's input voltage range covers your worst-case low and high voltages, and that its output matches your equipment (usually 220V or 230V).

Conclusion

Size by total load plus surge factor, add margin, and match phase and voltage. A correctly sized stabilizer protects equipment for years with zero maintenance.
