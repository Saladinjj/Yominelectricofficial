Step-Up and Step-Down Transformers: Voltage Conversion, Isolation and Selection Guide

ET
ET Engineering Team 9 min read September 12, 2026

A 110V appliance imported from the United States will not survive being plugged into a 220V European or Middle Eastern socket. A step-up or step-down transformer is the standard, reliable fix — and understanding how to size and specify one correctly is the difference between a safe installation and a burned transformer.

Step-up vs step-down: the basic difference

Both types work on the same transformer principle: a changing magnetic flux through a shared iron core links two coils of wire and induces a voltage proportional to the turns ratio. A step-down transformer has more turns on the primary (high-voltage side) than the secondary, so the output voltage is lower. A step-up transformer has more turns on the secondary, so the output voltage is higher. Many portable units like the ST-2000VA are bidirectional: switching the input to the 110V socket and using the 220V socket as output converts in one direction; reversing the connections converts the other way.

Common voltage conversion needs and the transformer you need

  • 110V ↔ 220V — the most common portable scenario; ST series covers 500VA to 10kVA
  • 220V ↔ 240V — buck-boost range, typically 100VA to 2kVA
  • 110V/120V ↔ 220V/230V/240V — bidirectional ST/TC series, all in one box
  • Isolation only (1:1) — BK / JBK control transformer series, 400VA to 2kVA

How to size a transformer in VA or kVA

The most common mistake is selecting a transformer that exactly matches the load wattage. Transformers must be derated for inductive loads (motors, compressors, air conditioners) and for starting currents. The safe formula:

  1. Sum all connected loads in watts.
  2. Divide by the load power factor (0.7–0.9 for mixed loads; use 0.8 if unknown).
  3. Multiply the result by 1.25 to give a 25% safety headroom.
  4. Round up to the next standard size.

Example: 1 air conditioner (900 W) + 1 laptop (200 W) + 2 lamps (100 W) = 1200 W total load. 1200 ÷ 0.8 × 1.25 = 1875 VA → choose ST-2000VA.

Isolation transformers: when voltage conversion isn't the goal

An isolation transformer provides galvanic separation — the primary and secondary share no direct electrical connection. Uses: medical-grade patient areas (IT power systems per IEC 60364-7-710), laboratory benches where earth faults must not propagate, audio/AV equipment free of ground loops, and data centre bypass circuits. The BK and JBK machine-tool control transformer series provides 400VA–5kVA 1:1 isolation with 36V, 42V, 110V or 220V secondary options for control circuits.

Built-in protection and safety features

Our ST and TC portable transformers include an integrated MCB (miniature circuit breaker) on the output side. The MCB trips on sustained overload or short-circuit within the connected equipment, protecting both the transformer and the appliances. The housing is ventilated sheet steel with a carrying handle for portability. All units are CE and ISO 9001 certified.

FAQ

What is a step-up transformer used for?

A step-up transformer increases voltage — most commonly to run 220V equipment from a 110V supply, or to boost low voltage on long cable runs.

How do I calculate the kVA size I need?

Sum all load watts, divide by power factor (0.8 if unknown), multiply by 1.25, and round up to the next standard VA/kVA size.

What is an isolation transformer?

An isolation transformer provides galvanic separation between primary and secondary — used in medical, laboratory and AV environments where ground faults must not propagate.

Can a step-up transformer run continuously?

Yes, if sized correctly. Running above the VA nameplate rating causes overheating. Our ST series includes an integrated MCB for overload protection.
