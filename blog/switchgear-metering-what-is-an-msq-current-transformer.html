Switchgear Metering: What Is an MSQ Current Transformer?

ET
ET Engineering Team October 20, 2026 10 min read

The Measurement Cornerstone for Modern Power Distribution and Energy Monitoring

Inside low-voltage main distribution boards (MDB), motor control centers (MCC), commercial tenant billing switchboards, and solar power feed-in panels, electric currents reaching thousands of amperes flow through heavy copper busbars.

To measure, monitor, and bill this electrical energy, digital multi-function power meters, smart energy analyzers, and overload protection devices require an accurate, scaled-down replica of the primary current.

Direct measurement is physically impossible: connecting a digital meter directly to a 1000A busbar would require massive internal shunts, generate extreme heat, and destroy delicate microprocessor electronics during minor system overloads.

The MSQ Series Low-Voltage Measuring Current Transformer (Model MSQ-30 to MSQ-125) represents the globally recognized engineering standard for low-voltage switchgear metering : an ultra-compact, precision-wound toroidal instrument transformer engineered to step down heavy primary currents to standardized 5A or 1A secondary signals with Class 0.5 revenue accuracy, complete galvanic isolation, and versatile dual mounting capabilities under IEC 61869-2.

Engineering Anatomy: Inside Model MSQ Measuring Current Transformers

  1. Grain-Oriented Silicon Steel (CRGO) Toroidal Core: Precision tape-wound magnetic core annealed in vacuum furnaces to achieve ultra-high magnetic permeability and negligible hysteresis loss, ensuring perfect linear measurement even at low load currents (5% to 20% of rated $I_n$).
  2. Flame-Retardant Polycarbonate/ABS Enclosure: Molded in robust, self-extinguishing plastic rated UL 94V-0, providing 3kV AC dielectric withstand protection between primary busbar and secondary terminals.
  3. Versatile Dual-Mode Mounting System: Equipped with built-in metal/plastic mounting feet for standard 35mm DIN rail mounting, alongside insulated brass tightening screws that lock the transformer securely directly onto the primary copper busbar.
  4. Sealable Transparent Secondary Terminal Cover: High-clarity polycarbonate cover enclosing secondary terminals S1 and S2, equipped with lead-wire sealing holes to prevent electricity theft and unauthorized meter tampering in commercial tenant billing.
  5. Standardized Metric Busbar Apertures: Rectangular window profiles precisely matched to international standard copper busbar dimensions ($30 imes 10\, ext{mm}$, $40 imes 10\, ext{mm}$, $60 imes 20\, ext{mm}$, $85 imes 30\, ext{mm}$, $100 imes 40\, ext{mm}$, $125 imes 50\, ext{mm}$) for clean, uncluttered cabinet aesthetics.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is an MSQ Current Transformer and why is it the global standard for low-voltage switchboard metering?

An MSQ Current Transformer—frequently designated in electrical panel manufacturing as a square-type measuring CT or low-voltage window CT—is a specialized instrument transformer housed in a compact square-profile flame-retardant casing designed to step down heavy primary AC currents (from 30A up to 6000A) to a standardized, safe 5A or 1A secondary measurement current. In electrical power distribution: <ul><li><strong>Safe Galvanic Isolation:</strong> Powers digital energy meters, power quality analyzers, and panel ammeters while isolating sensitive electronic circuitry from high-energy distribution busbars and lethal fault transients.</li><li><strong>Class 0.5 Precision Revenue Metering:</strong> Delivers strictly regulated ratio error ($\le \pm 0.5\%$) and phase displacement error across 100% to 120% of rated current under IEC 61869-2, ensuring exact commercial tenant submetering and utility billing accuracy.</li><li><strong>Compact Window Architecture:</strong> Designed with rectangular busbar window openings tailored to standard copper busbar dimensions (e.g., $30 imes 10\, ext{mm}$ in MSQ-30, $60 imes 20\, ext{mm}$ in MSQ-60, up to $125 imes 50\, ext{mm}$ in MSQ-125), allowing seamless, direct sliding over live busbars without cutting or jointing conductors.</li></ul>

What do the model numbers MSQ-30, MSQ-40, MSQ-60, and MSQ-85 signify?

The numeric suffix in the MSQ series designation directly indicates the maximum primary copper busbar width (in millimeters) that can pass through the central window aperture: <ul><li><strong>MSQ-30:</strong> Designed for primary currents from 30A to 300A; accommodates copper busbars up to $30 imes 10\, ext{mm}$ or cable diameters up to 30mm.</li><li><strong>MSQ-40:</strong> Designed for primary currents from 100A to 500A; accommodates busbars up to $40 imes 10\, ext{mm}$.</li><li><strong>MSQ-60:</strong> Designed for primary currents from 250A to 1000A; accommodates busbars up to $60 imes 20\, ext{mm}$.</li><li><strong>MSQ-85:</strong> Designed for primary currents from 400A to 1600A; accommodates busbars up to $85 imes 30\, ext{mm}$.</li><li><strong>MSQ-100 &amp; MSQ-125:</strong> Heavy-duty models handling 600A up to 6000A with large rectangular apertures up to $125 imes 50\, ext{mm}$.</li></ul>

Why is secondary open-circuit dangerous and how do MSQ transformers prevent it?

Under Lenz's Law, the secondary current in a CT creates a demagnetizing flux that opposes the primary flux. If the secondary circuit is accidentally opened while primary current flows, the opposing flux vanishes, and the entire primary ampere-turns drive the magnetic core into deep saturation. This induces lethal peak voltage spikes exceeding 2,000V to 5,000V across secondary terminals S1-S2, presenting fatal electrocution hazards to technicians and destroying transformer insulation. MSQ transformers feature shrouded secondary screw terminals with clear sealable protective covers, allowing test switches or shorting links to be safely installed before disconnecting any meter.
