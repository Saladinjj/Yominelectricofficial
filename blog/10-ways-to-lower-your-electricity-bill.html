10 Simple Ways to Lower Your Electricity Bill

ET
Engineering Team 6 min read August 12, 2026

Electricity bills keep rising, but the good news is that most households can cut theirs by 10 to 30 percent with a few simple habits. You do not need expensive gadgets or a complete home renovation — just a little knowledge and consistency. Here are ten practical ways to lower your electricity bill, in plain words that anyone can use.

1. Know What You Are Paying For

The first step is understanding your electricity bill: how many kilowatt-hours (kWh) you use, your price per unit, and which charges are fixed. You cannot reduce what you do not understand.

2. Switch Off Standby Devices

Televisions, chargers, and game consoles keep drawing small amounts of power even when "off." This phantom load can add up to 10 percent of your bill. Unplug devices or use a power strip with a switch.

3. Switch to LED Lighting

LED bulbs use about 80 percent less energy than old incandescent bulbs and last years longer. Replacing the bulbs you use most pays for itself within months.

4. Cook Smarter

Use lids on pots so water boils faster, match pan size to burner size, and batch-cook so you heat the oven once instead of many times.

5. Wash in Cold Water

Most of the energy a washing machine uses goes into heating water. Modern detergents clean perfectly in cold water, and your clothes last longer too.

6. Heat and Cool Wisely

For every degree you raise your air conditioner thermostat, you can save about 6 to 8 percent on cooling. Fans use far less power than AC, and closing curtains keeps heat out.

7. Use Big Appliances Off-Peak

If your tariff has cheaper night rates, run the washing machine, dishwasher, and water heater after peak hours. The same electricity, at a lower price.

8. Watch Your Meter

Check your electricity meter at the same time each week. If usage jumps without reason, something is wrong — a faulty appliance, a leak, or tampering. Early detection saves money.

9. Get a Smart Meter

Smart meters show your usage in real time, so you can see exactly which habits cost money. What gets measured gets managed — households with smart meters typically reduce consumption within months.

10. Seal the Small Leaks

Drafty doors and windows make heaters and coolers work harder. Simple weather stripping and door seals are cheap and cut both your electricity and your heating bill.

Why Small Changes Add Up Fast

None of these tips alone feels big, but together they compound. Cutting 10 percent of a monthly bill every month becomes a full month of free electricity every year. And when you combine good habits with an accurate meter and the right tariff, saving becomes automatic. If you are on an old meter, ask your utility about a prepaid or smart upgrade — many users save simply by seeing their balance every day.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much can I really save by turning off standby devices?

Standby or phantom loads typically account for 5 to 10 percent of a household's electricity use. Unplugging devices when not in use is one of the easiest savings with zero cost.

Is it worth replacing old appliances?

Often yes. A 15-year-old refrigerator can use twice the electricity of a modern energy-efficient model. If your appliance is old and failing, replacing it with an efficient model usually pays back within a few years.

Do solar panels lower my electricity bill?

Yes. Solar panels reduce the electricity you buy from the grid, and with net metering, you can earn credit for the power you export. See our beginners guide to solar for the details.
