What is a Power Analyzer?

ET
ET Engineering Team 8 min read August 19, 2026

A power analyzer is an advanced electronic instrument designed to measure and analyze various parameters of electrical power systems. Unlike simple meters, power analyzers provide a complete picture: voltage, current, active and reactive power, power factor, frequency, harmonics, and energy — all in one device. They are essential for energy audits, power quality studies, equipment efficiency testing, and industrial load analysis.

Key parameters a power analyzer measures

  • Voltage and current — RMS values per phase.
  • Active, reactive, and apparent power — W, var, VA.
  • Power factor — how effectively power is used.
  • Energy — kWh consumed over time.
  • Frequency — grid stability.
  • Harmonics and THD — distortion caused by non-linear loads.
  • Demand and load profiles — peaks and patterns over time.

Power analyzer vs energy meter

FeatureEnergy meterPower analyzer
Core jobBilling energy (kWh)Analyzing power quality and load
ParametersEnergy, some electrical dataFull electrical parameter set incl. harmonics
Typical useBilling, monitoringAudits, troubleshooting, efficiency tests
Data depthRegisters and totalsWaveforms, trends, event logs

Where power analyzers are used

  • Energy audits of factories, offices, and commercial buildings.
  • Power quality troubleshooting (voltage dips, harmonics, unbalance).
  • Motor and equipment efficiency measurement.
  • Verification of savings after efficiency projects.
  • Grid and substation monitoring in power distribution.

How a power analyzer works

The instrument samples voltage and current many thousands of times per second using internal transducers or external current transformers and Rogowski coils. DSP algorithms compute RMS values, powers, harmonics, and power factor, while memory and communication ports store and export the data for reporting.

FAQ

Do I need a power analyzer or an energy meter?

If you need billing or basic monitoring, an energy meter is enough. If you need to analyze why power quality is poor or where losses occur, use a power analyzer.

Can a power analyzer replace a CT-operated meter?

For measurement studies, yes. For legal billing, a certified energy meter is still required.

What is THD?

Total Harmonic Distortion — a measure of how much the waveform deviates from a clean sine wave due to non-linear loads.
