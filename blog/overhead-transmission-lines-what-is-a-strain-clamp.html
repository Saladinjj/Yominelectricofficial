Overhead Transmission Lines: What Is a Strain Clamp?

ET
ET Engineering Team October 15, 2026 10 min read

The Mechanical Anchor Holding High-Voltage Transmission Lines in Mid-Air

Across national high-voltage transmission grids and regional power distribution networks spanning deserts, mountain ranges, river valleys, and coastal corridors at 11kV, 33kV, 66kV, and 110kV, overhead conductors hang suspended under staggering mechanical tension.

Each kilometer of heavy bare Aluminum Conductor Steel Reinforced (ACSR) or All-Aluminum Alloy Conductor (AAAC) weighs thousands of kilograms. Under severe winter windstorms and heavy radial ice accumulation, mechanical tensile forces pulling on terminal structures reach tens of thousands of newtons.

While standard suspension clamps hang vertically to cradle the conductor's downward weight along straight-line runs, they are physically incapable of resisting longitudinal line pull. If a dead-end conductor were supported by a suspension clamp, the conductor would violently rip free, collapsing adjacent towers like dominos.

The High-Voltage Transmission Bolted Strain Clamp (Model NLL Series) serves as the indispensable structural anchor: a heavy-duty, high-strength aluminum alloy tension clamp engineered to grip bare overhead conductors with unshakeable mechanical holding power (≥ 95% RTS) while transferring tension safely into the tower crossarm.

Engineering Anatomy: Inside a Model NLL Bolted Aluminum Strain Clamp

  1. High-Strength Cast Aluminum Alloy Body: Gravity die-cast from electrical-grade A356 aluminum alloy with T6 heat treatment. Possesses exceptional tensile strength, zero magnetic hysteresis power loss, and natural corrosion immunity in salt-fog marine and industrial sulfur environments.
  2. Wave-Contoured Clamping Bed: The internal conductor groove features an engineered gentle sinusoidal wave profile that maximizes frictional holding grip via capstan friction principles without localized stress concentration.
  3. Heavy-Duty High-Tensile U-Bolts: Forged from Grade 8.8 structural steel with hot-dip galvanized protective coating exceeding 85 μm thickness. Paired with heavy spring lock washers and double locking nuts to prevent vibration loosening under wind aeolian vibration.
  4. Smooth Flared Bell-Mouth Keeper: Aluminum keeper plate designed with generously radiused bell-mouth entrances that eliminate sharp edge pinching, protecting conductor outer strands from fatigue cracking during wind swing.
  5. Integrated Clevis Tongue & Cotter Pin Assembly: Forged pulling eye connects directly to composite suspension insulator strings via standard clevis shackles, locked in place by a marine-grade stainless steel humped cotter pin.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a Strain Clamp and where is it installed along overhead transmission lines?

A Strain Clamp—frequently designated as a dead-end strain clamp, bolted tension clamp, or transmission anchoring clamp—is a specialized heavy-duty mechanical line fitting designed to securely grip a bare overhead transmission conductor and anchor it to a tension insulator string, transferring the full continuous longitudinal tensile pull of the line to the tower structure. Along high-voltage transmission and distribution corridors (11kV to 220kV+): <ul><li><strong>1. Dead-End &amp; Terminal Towers:</strong> Installed where an overhead transmission line terminates at a high-voltage substation switchyard gantry or transitions into underground high-voltage cables. The clamp anchors the entire mechanical line tension while a non-tensioned jumper loop carries the electrical current into the station busbar.</li><li><strong>2. Angle &amp; Corner Towers:</strong> Positioned wherever a transmission line changes horizontal direction by more than 15 to 30 degrees. Suspension clamps cannot withstand massive lateral resultant vector forces; tension insulator strings with dual strain clamps must be installed on both sides of the tower crossarm.</li><li><strong>3. Long-Span River &amp; Canyon Crossings:</strong> Secures conductors across extreme aerial spans (exceeding 500 to 1500 meters) where high wind speeds, heavy ice accumulation, and massive conductor sag generate tens of thousands of kilograms of mechanical tension.</li></ul>

How does a Bolted Strain Clamp achieve over 95% holding strength without crushing aluminum strands?

Securing a bare ACSR conductor under tens of thousands of newtons of tension requires a delicate balance between mechanical grip friction and conductor preservation: <ul><li><strong>Wave-Profile Curvature Groove:</strong> The internal clamping channel of YOMIN NLL series clamps features an engineered sinusoidal or wave-shaped contour. As the conductor passes through the curved bed, the normal force applied by the U-bolts generates massive frictional resistance along the conductor length via the capstan effect, rather than relying on point-crushing pressure.</li><li><strong>Radiused Aluminum Keeper Plate:</strong> The keeper plate is cast with smooth, flared radius bell-mouth ends. When linemen torque the high-tensile U-bolts to specified torque values (e.g. 45–60 N&middot;m), the clamping force distributes evenly across the outer aluminum strands without pinching, galling, or shearing individual wires.</li><li><strong>95% RTS Certification:</strong> Under formal IEC 61284 tensile testing, YOMIN strain clamps sustain mechanical holding loads exceeding 95% of the conductor's ultimate breaking strength without slippage for over 60 seconds.</li></ul>

Why are Bolted Aluminum Strain Clamps preferred over Hydraulic Compression Clamps in distribution networks?

While hydraulic compression strain clamps (NY series) provide permanent crimped joints for ultra-high-voltage (400kV–765kV) lines, <strong>Bolted Aluminum Strain Clamps (NLL series)</strong> dominate distribution and sub-transmission networks (11kV to 110kV) due to major operational advantages: <ul><li><strong>Tool-Free Simplicity in the Field:</strong> Bolted clamps require only a standard lineman socket wrench or torque wrench. They eliminate the need to haul heavy, multi-ton hydraulic compression pumps, gasoline generators, and specialized crimping dies up transmission poles.</li><li><strong>Reusability &amp; Line Sag Adjustability:</strong> During line stringing and maintenance, linemen can loosen the U-bolts, adjust conductor sag, re-tension the line, or re-route spans without cutting the conductor. Compression clamps are permanent and must be completely cut out if adjustments are needed.</li><li><strong>Zero Conductor Core Damage:</strong> Unlike hydraulic crimping which requires stripping outer aluminum layers to crimp the inner steel core, bolted clamps grip the fully assembled conductor directly over aluminum armor tape.</li></ul>
