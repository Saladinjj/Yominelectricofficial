Screw Machine Cost: What Drives the Price of an Automated Line

ET
ET Engineering Team October 28, 2026 8 min read

Two suppliers answer the same request for quotation for a “screw machine” and the numbers come back three times apart. Neither is being dishonest. They are describing different machines: one is a single screw-driving station, the other is a multi-stage line that cuts, drills, taps, drives and inspects. In this category price is not a market figure you can look up. It is the sum of the operations you ask the machine to perform.

First, decide which screw machine you are buying

In North American usage a screw machine usually means a CNC turning machine that produces small turned parts from bar stock. In busbar and terminal production the same phrase means an automatic screw-driving machine, or a line that prepares a metal bar and then assembles it. The two are unrelated purchases with unrelated price structures, and a quote cannot be compared across them.

YOMIN builds the second kind: an integrated line that starts with a brass or aluminium busbar row and ends with an assembled, tested part. If your requirement was written for turned parts, this is the wrong category and no amount of price comparison will help. If you produce busbar assemblies and terminal blocks, the four blocks below are what your quotation is actually made of.

The four blocks that set the price

Cost blockWhat it coversHow it moves the quote
Operation scopeCutting, drilling, tapping, chamfering, screw driving, testing, finishingEvery operation is either its own station or an extra re-fixture of the same part
Station count and layoutOne compound station versus a multi-stage line with transfer between stagesMore stations mean more structure, more spindles and more engineering hours
Control packagePLC and touch screen per station, recipes, interlocks, fault displayPush-button control and recipe-driven PLC control are different products
Tooling, fixtures and acceptanceJaws and fixtures per drawing, screw spindles, test station, spare toolingFrequently the largest optional line item, and the one most often left out

The frame is rarely the variable. Two lines can use comparable steel and still differ by a factor of two, because the money sits in spindles, transfer mechanisms, PLC logic and the fixtures that hold your specific bar.

Station count is the biggest single lever

A single-station screw driver does one thing: it drives screws into a bar that an operator has already drilled and loaded by hand. A three-stage line takes the operator's hands out of every step. That difference, not the tonnage of steel, is what separates a low-cost machine from a production line.

Why “same machine, half the price” usually is not

When a quotation lands far below the others, the difference is almost always visible in the specification rather than in the supplier's margin. The usual places to look:

  • Fewer spindles. A machine that drives one screw at a time costs less than one that drives a pattern simultaneously, and takes proportionally longer per part.
  • Manual changeover. No recipe storage means every product change is a mechanical reset with a skilled fitter standing next to the machine.
  • No test station. Scrap is not removed, it is deferred: it surfaces at the customer, where it costs far more than the station would have.
  • Unspecified spares. A spindle or driver bit sourced only from the original builder turns a two-day stoppage into a six-week one.
  • No acceptance criteria. Without an agreed sample run, a torque audit or a cycle-time test, “accepted” means nothing more than “delivered”.

Buy or outsource: the calculation that decides

The comparison is not machine price against an hourly labour rate. It is the annual cost of the current process against the annual cost of the line, where the current process includes direct labour, rework, scrap and the overtime that appears whenever a container is late.

Put four numbers on one page: parts per year by product family, minutes of manual work per part today, fully loaded labour cost per hour, and the scrap plus rework percentage. Multiply the first three and add the cost of the fourth. That annual figure is what the machine competes against, together with tooling, maintenance and the floor space it occupies.

If a line removes 60 % of the manual minutes, the payback period is the machine quotation divided by 60 % of that annual figure. Anything that shortens changeover or removes a second operation also removes inspection labour, which is why station count usually matters more to the business case than the headline cycle time.

What to put in the RFQ

Suppliers quote what they are given. These eight items separate a comparable quotation from a guess:

  • A dimensional drawing of the part, with tolerances and the datum you inspect from.
  • Material and temper, stated explicitly rather than as “brass” or “aluminium”.
  • Bar cross-section, thickness and the length range you will actually run.
  • Hole pattern, drill and tap sizes, and whether double-row holes are required.
  • Screw type, size, and the torque target with its tolerance.
  • Target cycle time and annual volume by family, including the smallest batch you will accept running.
  • Electrical supply: voltage, phases and frequency at the installation point.
  • What “accepted” means: sample run quantity, torque audit method, cycle-time measurement.

Frequently asked questions

Why is there no list price for a screw machine?

Because the machine is configured around your part. Station count, spindle layout, fixtures, test scope and control level all change with the drawing, so a list price would only be accurate for a customer who does not exist yet. A quotation follows a drawing and a volume.

Is an automatic screw machine the same as a CNC screw machine?

No. A CNC screw machine turns small parts from bar stock by subtractive machining. An automatic screw-driving machine, or a processing line like the YM-SM-001, prepares a busbar and assembles it with screws. The terminology overlaps; the equipment does not.

How many stations do I actually need?

One per operation that cannot be completed in the same fixture without losing the datum. Cutting, drilling, tapping and chamfering can share a compound station; screw driving usually needs its own, because the part orientation and the torque reaction are different.

Can the line be built to my busbar drawing?

Yes. Customization is per drawing or sample, including hole sizes, aluminium profiles and double-row hole patterns. Send the drawing with the tolerance and the datum you inspect from.

What information speeds up a quotation?

The drawing, the material and temper, the annual volume by family, the target cycle time and the electrical supply at the installation point. With those five, a station layout can be proposed without a site visit.

Pricing a line for your busbar or terminal family?

Send the drawing, the material, the annual volume and the target cycle time. We will come back with a station layout and a configuration-based quotation.
