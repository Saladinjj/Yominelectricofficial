Utility Service Entrance Protection: What Is a House Service Cutout Fuse?

ET
ET Engineering Team October 3, 2026 10 min read

The Primary Gateway of Electric Utility Power: The House Service Cutout

Every home, commercial storefront, and light industrial workshop connected to the low-voltage public power grid has an electrical service entrance. Yet for many property owners and facilities managers, the most critical electrical safety component in their building is completely overlooked: the House Service Cutout Fuse.

Known across the United Kingdom, the Middle East, the Caribbean, Africa, and international Commonwealth export markets as the service head, company fuse, or Series 7 cutout, this robust enclosure is the first line of defense protecting customer premises against catastrophic grid short-circuits.

Manufactured from flame-retardant thermoset DMC or Bakelite, the house service cutout houses the utility's high-breaking-capacity (HBC) cartridge fuse, providing fault isolation and a legal point of isolation between the public utility distribution network operator (DNO) and the private electrical installation.

Engineering Anatomy: Inside a Heavy-Duty Service Cutout

A certified BS 1361 / IEC 60269-1 service cutout consists of four essential subsystems:

  1. Thermoset Insulating Housing: Molded from glass-fiber reinforced Dough Moulding Compound (DMC) or high-impact phenolic Bakelite. It possesses exceptional tracking resistance, heat deflection temperatures exceeding 150°C, and self-extinguishing UL 94 V-0 flame ratings.
  2. High-Breaking Capacity (HBC) Fuse Carrier: A removable pull-out carrier that houses an enclosed cylindrical cartridge fuse (BS 1361 Type II, dimensions 57 mm × 22 mm). Filled with high-purity silica quartz sand, the fuse element quenches short-circuit arc energies up to 33,000 Amperes safely without exploding.
  3. Solid Brass Terminal Blocks: Heavy electro-tinned brass tunnel blocks equipped with mechanical shear-head screws or serrated clamping plates, capable of accepting both copper and aluminum solid or stranded cables from 6 mm² up to 35 mm².
  4. Tamper-Evident Anti-Theft Sealing: Precision-molded sealing holes accommodate utility stainless steel or nylon security wire seals, legally barring unauthorized access and eliminating non-technical energy theft.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a House Service Cutout (or Service Head Cutout) and where is it located in an electrical installation?

A House Service Cutout—frequently referred to by electrical contractors and utility distribution engineers as the main service head, service cutout fuse, or supply cutout—is the primary electrical intake device that marks the boundary between the public electric utility network and a private building's internal electrical installation. Physically, the cutout is installed inside the intake cupboard or external meter cabinet immediately before the utility kWh electricity meter. The heavy incoming concentric or split-concentric supply cable from the street underground main connects directly into the bottom terminals of the cutout. Inside, the phase conductor passes through a high-breaking-capacity (HBC) cylindrical fuse link (typically 60A, 80A, or 100A). The output of the cutout then feeds directly into the electric meter, which subsequently feeds the consumer unit (distribution board). Because it is located upstream of the meter, the cutout and its fuse are the legal property of the Distribution Network Operator (DNO).

What is the difference between a 60A, 80A, and 100A service cutout fuse rating, and why are utilities upgrading to 100A?

The rating of the service cutout determines the maximum continuous electrical power capacity the property can safely draw from the grid: <ul><li><strong>60A Cutout Fuse (&approx; 14 kW):</strong> Standard for older residential homes built between 1950 and 1980 with basic electrical lighting and appliances.</li><li><strong>80A Cutout Fuse (&approx; 18.5 kW):</strong> Standard upgrade implemented in the 1990s and 2000s for homes with electric showers, electric ovens, and air conditioning.</li><li><strong>100A Cutout Fuse (&approx; 23 kW):</strong> The modern utility standard required for modern electrified homes. Utilities across the UK, Europe, and the Middle East are executing massive upgrade programs to replace 60A and 80A fuses with 100A cutouts to handle simultaneous heavy electrical loads such as 7.4 kW Level-2 EV home chargers, heat pumps, solar inverter export, and induction cooktops.</li></ul>Crucially, upgrading from 60A to 100A requires the utility to verify that the underground supply cable is at least 25 mm&sup2; or 35 mm&sup2; copper to prevent cable overheating under sustained full-load demand.

Why must a House Service Cutout be fitted with tamper-evident utility wire seals?

Because the cutout is situated upstream of the billing meter, tapping into the terminals of the cutout allows unmetered electricity consumption (power theft). Furthermore, pulling the fuse carrier under heavy load poses severe electric arc flash hazards. Tamper-evident lead or polycarbonate wire seals prevent unauthorized individuals from opening the carrier to steal power, bypass the meter, or replace blown fuses with dangerous copper wire makeshift links. Only licensed utility technicians or authorized emergency contractors are legally permitted to cut the seal and pull the fuse carrier.
