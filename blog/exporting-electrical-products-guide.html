The Complete Guide to Exporting Electrical Products from China

LG
LG Logistics Team 8 min read July 22, 2026

Importing electrical products — meters, transformers, sockets, busbars — from China is a proven supply route, but only if you handle certifications, documents and shipping correctly. This guide walks through the complete process from factory selection to arrival at your warehouse, based on how Yomin Electric ships to 95+ countries.

Step 1: Choose a Reliable Factory

  • Verify the factory's export history and licenses (business license, export qualification).
  • Ask for test reports and certificates for the exact models you plan to buy.
  • Check the factory's track record on Alibaba.com reviews and third-party audits (SGS, BV, TUV).
  • Order samples first and test them under your local voltage and frequency (230 V/50 Hz, 110 V/60 Hz, etc.).
  • Confirm the factory can produce your label, manual language and packaging requirements.

Step 2: Get the Certifications Right

MarketTypical requirements
European UnionCE marking, IEC 62053 / EN 50470 compliance, RoHS
USA / CanadaUL or ETL listing, ANSI C12 metering standards
Middle East / AfricaIEC standards; local type approval for utility meters
Latin AmericaIEC-based standards; some countries require local certification
Southeast AsiaIEC standards; utility-specific approvals

Electrical products are regulated in most markets. Confirm before ordering whether you need CE, UL/ETL, or local utility type approval — retrofitting certification after arrival is expensive and slow. Yomin Electric provides CE, IEC and ANSI-compliant products with test certificates on request.

Step 3: Documentation Pack

  • Commercial invoice — seller, buyer, description, unit prices, total value.
  • Packing list — carton count, weights, dimensions, HS codes per item.
  • Bill of lading (B/L) or air waybill — the transport document; original B/L is needed for L/C payment.
  • Certificate of origin — often required for duty preference or customs clearance.
  • Test reports & certificates — CE/UL certificates, test reports for the customs broker and end customer.
  • Insurance certificate — recommended for sea freight.

Step 4: Shipping and Incoterms

Common arrangements for electrical goods from China:

  • FOB — the factory delivers to the port; you arrange the ocean freight.
  • EXW — you collect from the factory; lowest product price, most logistics work.
  • DDP — the seller handles freight, insurance, customs and delivery to your door; highest price, least hassle.

Sea freight (LCL or FCL) is the norm for meters and transformers; air freight suits samples and urgent spare parts. Typical sea transit: 15–25 days to most ports, plus 2–7 days of factory production time. Yomin Electric quotes FOB Shanghai/Ningbo or DDP depending on your preference.

Step 5: Customs, Duties and HS Codes

Your customs broker clears goods under the correct HS code for each product. Meters, transformers, sockets and busbars have different codes and duty rates, so a precise packing list matters. Work with a broker in your country before the shipment leaves the factory, and keep the certificates handy — customs frequently asks for them on electrical goods.

Step 6: Payment Terms

  • T/T (bank transfer) — 30% deposit, 70% before shipment is standard for first orders.
  • L/C (letter of credit) — for large orders; protects both sides via banks.
  • Trade Assurance — Alibaba.com's escrow-style protection for covered orders.
  • PayPal / card — for samples and small first orders.

Never pay 100% in advance to an unverified supplier. Use Trade Assurance or an L/C for significant first orders.

Step 7: Packaging That Survives the Trip

  • Meters and instruments: individual foam or blister protection, 5-layer cartons, palletized with stretch wrap.
  • Busbars and heavy metal parts: wooden crates, edge protection, anti-rust paper.
  • Mark cartons with model, quantity, HS code and handling marks.
  • Add desiccant for humid routes (sea containers sweat badly).
  • Confirm your label, language and logo requirements are printed at the factory — re-labelling abroad costs more than the product.

FAQ

Do I need an import license for electrical meters?

Most countries require some form of type approval or certification for utility meters; industrial components are usually easier. Check with your customs broker and the local utility before committing to a large order.

What is the minimum order quantity from Yomin Electric?

MOQs vary by product — many meter models start at small trial orders, while custom-labelled production typically requires a few hundred units. Ask our sales team for the exact MOQ for your models.

How long does a full order take?

Typically 7–15 days production plus 15–25 days sea freight — about 3–6 weeks door-to-door for most destinations, depending on customs.

Conclusion

Exporting electrical products is straightforward when you control the five critical points: a verifiable factory, correct certification for your market, a complete document pack, clear Incoterms and payment terms, and packaging designed for sea freight.
