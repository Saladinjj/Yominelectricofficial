What Is Fuse

ET
ET Engineering Team November 17, 2026 10 min read

A fuse is a short length of metal, deliberately chosen to melt before anything else in the circuit is damaged. It is the simplest protective device that exists: no mechanism, no trip coil, no settings to drift. The element heats in proportion to the square of the current, and when the heat cannot escape fast enough the element melts, an arc forms, and the arc is quenched inside a body packed with silica or another filler.

That is the whole principle, and it explains both why fuses are trusted and why they are so often misapplied. A fuse cannot be adjusted, so it protects exactly what its curve says and nothing else. Get the curve right and the protection is absolute. Get the rated current right and the curve wrong, and the installation has a device that fits the holder, passes inspection, and fails to protect the cable.

What actually happens when a fuse operates

Four events occur in sequence, and the sequence explains most of the specification language.

  • Heating. The element carries load current continuously. It is designed to run hot but not to melt at rated current.
  • Melting. At some multiple of rated current the element reaches its melting point. The time this takes is the pre-arcing time.
  • Arcing. The melted element vaporises and draws an arc. The filler absorbs energy, cools the arc and builds pressure until the arc cannot sustain itself.
  • Clearing. Current stops. Melting time plus arcing time is the total clearing time, and the total energy let through is the clearing I²t.

Because a fuse interrupts by destroying itself, it is a single-use device. That is the trade against a breaker: the fuse is faster, cheaper and needs no maintenance, but it must be replaced after every operation.

The four ratings that decide everything

Only one of these is printed large on the barrel. All four matter.

RatingWhat it meansWhat goes wrong if you ignore it
Rated voltageThe maximum system voltage the fuse is qualified to interruptAn arc that will not extinguish, and a body that ruptures
Rated currentContinuous current at the reference ambient, not a fault ratingNuisance operation, or a cable left unprotected
Breaking capacityThe largest fault current it can safely interruptThe fuse bursts instead of clearing, which is a fire risk
Clearing I²tEnergy let through during operationA cable or busbar damaged before the fuse opens

Breaking capacity is the rating most often missing from an enquiry. A fuse chosen for its amperage alone may be installed where the prospective fault current exceeds what its body can interrupt. The result is not a graceful failure.

A body designed for a blade base cannot be substituted for a screw-in or bolt-in form whatever the label says about amperage. The base decides the form long before the current does.

Fuse or breaker

Both protect. They differ in what happens after they operate, and in how precisely they can be coordinated.

FuseBreaker
Operating speedVery fast at high fault current, current limitingSlower, and limited by the mechanism
After operationReplace the fuseReset the lever
SettingsFixed by the element, cannot driftAdjustable, so they can be set wrong
CoordinationPredictable from published curvesDepends on trip unit and settings
Cost at high ratingLower per wayHigher, and the frame size jumps

The practical rule in industrial panels is that fuses win where fault levels are high and predictable coordination matters, and breakers win where a reset without a stock of spares matters more. Many boards use both, with the fuse doing the current limiting duty.

Choosing one in five steps

  • System voltage and, for DC, the array voltage. A DC arc does not self-extinguish, so a DC duty fuse is a different device, not a derated one.
  • Prospective fault current. This sets the minimum breaking capacity, and it is measured or calculated, not guessed.
  • Load type. A motor needs an aM fuse plus an overload relay; a cable needs gG. Mixing them removes protection that nobody will notice until a fault.
  • Cable size. Check the total clearing I²t against the conductor's withstand, not just the current rating.
  • Ambient and enclosure. A fuse inside a sealed box runs above the curve's reference conditions and opens earlier than the published line.

Specification checklist

  • Voltage, AC or DC, and the frequency for AC duty.
  • Breaking capacity at or above the prospective fault current.
  • Class marking written as gG, aM or gPV on the order, not left to the supplier's stock.
  • Clearing I²t checked against the cable or busbar withstand.
  • Body form and base, since the holder decides what will physically fit.
  • Ambient, because a sealed enclosure changes the effective rating of every fuse inside it.

Send the fault level and the class with the amperage. A quotation on current alone will be the right size in the wrong device.

Frequently asked questions

What is a fuse in one sentence?

A fuse is a metal element that melts to open the circuit before the cable, the busbar or the equipment behind it is damaged, then has to be replaced.

What is the difference between a fuse and a circuit breaker?

A fuse destroys itself to clear a fault and must be replaced; a breaker trips and can be reset. A fuse is faster at high fault current and its characteristic cannot drift, while a breaker is more convenient to restore.

Why does the breaking capacity matter as much as the amperage?

Because the amperage only says what current it carries. If the prospective fault current at that point exceeds the breaking capacity, the fuse ruptures instead of clearing the fault, which is a fire and arc-flash risk.

Can I use an AC fuse on a DC solar circuit?

No. A DC arc does not cross zero and does not self-extinguish, so a PV string needs a fuse rated and tested for DC duty at the array voltage. An AC-rated fuse has never been qualified to interrupt that fault.

Choosing a fuse for a board or a PV string?

Send the system voltage, the prospective fault current, the load type and the cable size. We will confirm the class and the body form that fit the position.
