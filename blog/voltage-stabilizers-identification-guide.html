Voltage Stabilizers Explained: Pick the Right One in 5 Minutes

ET Engineering Team 7 min read · September 1, 2026

Bad voltage kills equipment quietly: refrigerators that die young, CNC machines that trip, clinic equipment that flickers. A stabilizer is the cheapest insurance in the building — but only if you buy the right type. The chart above shows six of our most-shipped stabilizers and regulators; here's how to tell them apart without an engineering degree.

The Two Big Families: Servo vs Relay

Servo motor stabilizers (like our TND/SVC single phase and TNS three phase series) use a copper-wound transformer and a servo motor that continuously trims the output. Result: tight accuracy (±1–3%), smooth correction — the choice for sensitive and industrial loads.

Relay-type regulators switch transformer taps in steps. They're lighter, cheaper and fast enough for homes, TVs, fridges and AC units. If the budget is tight and the load is domestic, relay is fine.

The 6 Units on the Chart

Single phase servo stabilizer (TND/SVC, 10–20 kVA)

The workshop standard: copper servo motor, LED display, 110/230 V output. Clinics, small factories, labs.

Three phase AVR (TNS series)

Whole-building and machine-line protection, fully automatic, 50 Hz. When a factory says "our voltage sags at peak hours", this is the unit.

Mini single phase regulator (1000 W)

The pocket-sized one for TVs, computers and small appliances — a best-seller for distributors in unstable-grid markets.

Wall mounted AVR (3 kVA, digital display)

Relay type with a clean digital face, made to sit next to an AC unit or home office. Installers love it because it mounts like a breaker box.

Three phase 15 kVA stabilizer

Mid-size industrial TND/SVC — the sweet spot for workshops, printing machines and small production lines.

Industrial AVR 30 kVA (relay type)

Heavy domestic/commercial capacity at relay economics: fully automatic AC regulation for larger homes, shops and light industry.

FAQ

Servo or relay for air conditioners?

Relay is usually enough for a single AC unit; choose servo if the supply swings widely or the AC is part of a sensitive installation (server room, clinic).

How do I size a stabilizer?

Add up the load in watts, multiply by ~1.25 for motor starting headroom, and pick the next kVA step up. Send us your appliance list and we'll do the math with you.

Do you make custom input voltage ranges?

Yes — wide-range versions for weak grids are a standard OEM option. Specify your local grid (e.g. 140–260 V) with the order.

Conclusion
