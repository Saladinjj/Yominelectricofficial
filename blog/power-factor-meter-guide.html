Power Factor Meters: How to Measure, Interpret and Improve Your Electrical Power Factor

ET
ET Engineering Team 7 min read September 12, 2026

Every electrical meter displays kWh — but the power factor display is the one that tells you whether your installation is costing you more than it should. A low power factor means you are paying for apparent power that your equipment doesn't actually convert to work. Understanding what the PF reading means, and choosing the right meter to track it, is one of the highest-ROI instrumentation decisions a facility manager can make.

What power factor actually measures

In an AC circuit, voltage and current can be out of phase with each other. When current lags behind voltage (inductive load — motors, transformers, fluorescent ballasts), the product of V × I at any instant includes a reactive component that flows back and forth without doing work. Power factor is the cosine of the phase angle φ between voltage and current: PF = cos(φ) = Real Power (W) / Apparent Power (VA). A purely resistive load (electric heater) has PF = 1.0. A motor running at light load might have PF = 0.6.

Why utilities charge for low power factor

Utilities must size their cables, transformers and switchgear for apparent power (kVA), not just the kilowatts you actually use. When your PF is 0.7, the utility carries 43% more current than a unity-PF customer using the same real power. Most commercial tariffs include a reactive energy (kvarh) charge or a minimum PF clause (typically 0.9 or 0.95 lagging). Falling below the threshold triggers a penalty multiplier on your monthly bill.

Dedicated PF meter vs multifunction energy meter

A dedicated panel-mount power factor meter shows a live PF reading — useful for monitoring a specific motor or transformer on the panel door. It does not record energy, generate demand reports or provide RS485 data output.

A multifunction energy meter (YEM051, DTSD666-7P) provides all of the above in a single DIN-rail or panel-mount device: active energy (kWh), reactive energy (kvarh), power factor, voltage, current, frequency, demand and harmonics — with Modbus-RTU RS485 output for SCADA and BMS integration. For any installation where you need to track energy cost, log data or communicate with a building management system, the multifunction meter is the correct choice.

FAQ

What does a power factor meter measure?

It measures the ratio of real power (W) to apparent power (VA) — showing how efficiently the electrical load is converting current into useful work.

What is a good power factor reading?

Above 0.95 is good for most commercial and industrial applications. Most utilities charge penalties below 0.85–0.90.

Do I need a dedicated PF meter or a multifunction meter?

For most installations, a multifunction meter (kWh + kvarh + PF + RS485) replaces both a kWh meter and any separate PF instrument — at a lower combined cost.
