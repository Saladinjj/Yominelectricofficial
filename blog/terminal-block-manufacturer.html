Terminal Block Manufacturer: How to Specify and Qualify a DIN Rail Supplier

ET
ET Engineering Team November 23, 2026 9 min read

A terminal block manufacturer is qualified on four figures and one sample, not on a catalogue PDF. The four figures are rated voltage, rated current, wire range and pitch; the sample is what tells you whether the brass, the plating and the clamp actually hold the wire you intend to use. Because a terminal block costs cents, buyers often skip that work — and then discover the problem at the panel, where a block that will not accept a 6 mm² ferrule stops a production line.

Screw clamp or spring clamp: decide this first

Everything downstream — the pitch, the accessories, the wiring time, the maintenance procedure — follows from the clamping method. Choose it before you choose a supplier.

  • Screw clamp. A screw drives a clamp onto the conductor. It accepts a wide range of conductor types with one part number, it can be re-tightened, and every electrician already knows how to use it. It also needs a torque check in the maintenance schedule, and a poorly tightened screw is a hot joint waiting to happen.
  • Spring clamp. A spring holds the conductor. Wiring is faster, the contact force does not depend on the installer, and vibration does not loosen it. It needs the correct tool to open, and fine-stranded conductors need a ferrule.

For a panel built once and rarely touched, screw clamp is usually the pragmatic answer. For a machine that vibrates, or a site where wiring labour is the dominant cost, spring clamp pays for itself. Do not mix the two inside one terminal strip: the pitches differ and the accessories will not line up.

The four figures that define the part

Put these on the drawing or the purchase order. If any one is missing, the supplier will choose a value for you, and it will be the value that suits their stock.

FigureWhat it controlsWhat goes wrong when it is unstated
Rated voltage The insulation distance and the housing material. It is a declared rating against the applicable standard, not a test the block survives. A block with adequate current but insufficient clearance for the system voltage, discovered at dielectric testing.
Rated current The conductor cross-section, the clamp force and the thermal behaviour of the assembly. Blocks that pass individually but derate once mounted side by side in a strip.
Wire range The smallest and largest conductor the clamp accepts — stated as a range, not a single size. The 6 mm² ferrule that does not enter, or the 0.5 mm² wire that is not gripped.
Pitch Width per pole in millimetres, which sets the strip length, the DIN rail length and accessory compatibility. End stops, jumpers and marker tags that do not fit the strip you built.

DIN rail terminal blocks are normally declared against IEC 60947-7-1. Ask for the declaration covering the exact series you are buying, not the family it belongs to.

Materials: where the cost and the service life sit

Two terminal blocks can look identical in a photograph and differ entirely in what they are made of. YOMIN's DIN rail terminal blocks use a brass current-carrying body with copper inlet and outlet, in a PA or PC housing.

ElementWhat it doesWhat to ask
Brass body Carries the current and takes the clamp load. Brass machines well and holds a thread reliably. The alloy and whether the thread is cut or formed, because a formed thread holds better in thin sections.
Copper inlet and outlet The contact surfaces where the conductor seats. Copper conducts better than brass at the contact. Whether the copper is a separate insert or a plating, and its thickness if plated.
PA or PC housing Insulation, flame behaviour and mechanical retention of the block on the rail. The flammability class and the comparative tracking index of the exact grade, since these decide the rated voltage you can declare.
Screw and clamp The force that keeps the joint cool over years of thermal cycling. The screw material and whether it is captive, because a lost screw in a live panel is a safety event.

The lesson for a buyer is that a quote far below the band is not a bargain — it is usually a thinner brass section or a lower-grade housing, and neither shows up until the thermal test.

How to qualify the manufacturer

  • Ask which parts are made in-house. The brass body, the thread and the insert are the parts that decide performance. A supplier that buys finished blocks and prints a box is a trader, and will not control the dimension that fails.
  • Ask for the drawing of the block you are buying, with the pitch and the wire range marked. If a supplier cannot supply a drawing, they do not control the part.
  • Ask for the standard declaration and the report behind it, for that series.
  • Ask what the minimum order is per variant and how a variant change affects tooling. Colour and marking changes are cheap; a body change is not.
  • Ask how the blocks are packed and counted. At these unit prices, a counting error in a 10,000-piece order is a real cost, and loose blocks damage their own threads in transit.

The sample tests worth running

You do not need a laboratory to reject a bad terminal block. These four checks on a handful of samples will separate a controlled part from a copied one.

CheckHow to run itWhat a pass looks like
Conductor acceptance Insert the smallest and largest conductor in your range, with the ferrule you actually use. Both enter without force and are gripped firmly; the largest does not splay the clamp.
Pull-out Clamp a conductor, then pull steadily by hand after the screw is torqued to your procedure. The conductor does not move, and the screw does not turn as you pull.
Thread and screw Drive and release the screw ten times on the same sample. The thread holds its torque on the tenth cycle; no swarf appears and the screw stays captive.
Assembly and rail fit Build a strip of ten and mount it on the rail you will actually use, with end stops and a jumper. Blocks seat without force, the strip is straight, and the accessories align with the pitch.

A supplier who has run these tests already will answer with numbers and photographs. One who has not will answer with adjectives.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between a terminal block manufacturer and a trader?

A manufacturer makes the current-carrying body, cuts or forms the thread and controls the pitch dimension, so it can supply a drawing and hold a tolerance. A trader buys finished blocks and resells them, so a variant change becomes a purchasing problem rather than a production one. YOMIN manufactures brass DIN rail terminal blocks with copper inlet and outlet in PA and PC housings, and quotes against your specification.

Which four figures must appear on the purchase order?

Rated voltage, rated current, wire range and pitch. Each one removes a decision the supplier would otherwise make for you: voltage sets the insulation distance, current sets the conductor and clamp, wire range sets what actually fits, and pitch sets whether the strip and its accessories assemble.

Why do terminal block prices vary so much between suppliers?

Because the visible shape is only part of the part. Brass alloy and section thickness, whether the thread is cut or formed, whether copper is a separate insert or a plating, and the flammability class and tracking index of the housing all move the cost. In the current YOMIN catalogue, brass DIN rail terminal blocks sit in bands from about US$0.01 to US$0.08 per piece, and configured connectors from about US$0.10 to US$0.50, which shows how much the specification, not the negotiation, sets the price.

Can I mix screw clamp and spring clamp blocks in one strip?

Not on the same strip. The two families use different pitches and different accessory sets, so jumpers, end stops and marker tags will not align across the boundary. Build two strips and join them with a partition, or standardise on one clamp type for the whole panel.
