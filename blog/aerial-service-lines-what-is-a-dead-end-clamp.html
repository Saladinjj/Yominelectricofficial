Line Hardware & ABC Fittings / Dead End Tension Clamps

Aerial Service Lines: What Is a Dead End Clamp?

ET
ET Engineering Team October 11, 2026 10 min read

The Mechanical Anchor for Low-Voltage Overhead Distribution Lines

Across urban residential neighborhoods, rural electrification networks, and commercial distribution zones worldwide, low-voltage aerial bundled cables (LV-ABC) operating at 230V / 400V deliver electrical power from pole-mounted transformers directly into homes and commercial facilities.

Along these distribution paths, branch service drop cables (typically 2-core or 4-core insulated aluminium conductors from 16 mm² to 50 mm²) must span distances of 20 to 50 meters between utility poles and building facades.

These service spans are subjected to continuous environmental mechanical stresses: high wind gusts, galloping oscillations, ice and snow accumulation, and thermal expansion cycles. If a service cable is improperly anchored, excessive sag damages building connections, while over-clamping crushes the cable insulation, resulting in violent short-circuit flashovers and power outages.

The Insulated Wedge Dead End Clamp (Model AC-O / S170 / S270 Series) provides the international utility-standard solution: a robust, tool-free mechanical anchoring fitting that grips 2-core and 4-core twisted ABC service cables safely, holding line tensions up to 3.5kN while preserving 100% of the cable's electrical insulation integrity.

Engineering Anatomy: Inside a Certified Wedge Dead End Service Clamp

  1. High-Strength Thermoplastic Body: Injection-molded from weather-resistant, UV-stabilized fiberglass-reinforced polyamide (nylon) that resists solar degradation, extreme frost (-40°C), and corrosive industrial atmospheres without embrittlement.
  2. Self-Adjusting Dual Insulating Wedges: Two precision-serrated wedges slide freely inside the conical clamp body. As line tension increases, the wedges draw inwards, automatically multiplying gripping force without requiring manual bolt tightening.
  3. Flexible Stainless Steel Wire Bail (AISI 304): A heavy-duty, corrosion-proof flexible wire bail hooks effortlessly into utility pole pigtail bolts, eye brackets, and facade hooks. Its flexible construction absorbs line vibrations and allows multi-directional swiveling.
  4. Wear-Resistant Plastic Bail Saddle Block: An insulating thermoplastic saddle insert prevents metallic point-friction wear between the stainless steel bail and the clamp body, ensuring decades of vibration-free service.
  5. Wide Cable Range Versatility: The self-aligning wedge geometry accommodates varying cable diameters effortlessly, securely holding 2x16 mm², 2x25 mm², 4x16 mm², 4x25 mm², 4x35 mm², and 4x50 mm² aerial service cables in a single unified fitting.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a Dead End Clamp and where is it installed on low-voltage overhead service networks?

A Dead End Clamp—frequently termed a service anchoring clamp, wedge dead end clamp, or terminal tension clamp—is a specialized electrical mechanical fitting designed to anchor, dead-end, and terminate insulated low-voltage aerial bundled cables (LV-ABC). It is installed at terminal points where the overhead electrical line ends or changes direction sharply, specifically: <ul><li><strong>1. Utility Distribution Pole Brackets:</strong> Where a 2-core or 4-core insulated service drop cable branches off the main overhead LV-ABC feeder line towards a consumer premise, hooked onto a pole pigtail hook or angle bracket.</li><li><strong>2. Customer Building Facades &amp; Meter Boxes:</strong> Where the incoming service drop cable arrives at the exterior wall of a residential home, commercial shop, or industrial facility, mounted to a wall anchor bracket just before the cable enters the revenue meter cabinet.</li><li><strong>3. Line Angle &amp; Dead-End Poles:</strong> At physical dead-ends or sharp corners (> 30 degrees) of overhead service routes where continuous mechanical tension must be transferred directly into the pole structure.</li></ul>

How does the self-locking wedge mechanism secure insulated ABC cables without damaging the outer sheath?

Traditional bolted friction clamps concentrate crushing mechanical forces on a small contact point, pinching through the XLPE insulation and causing dangerous phase-to-phase short circuits or water ingress into the stranded aluminium conductor. The YOMIN AC-O Dead End Clamp utilizes an advanced <strong>self-locking dual wedge design</strong>: <ul><li><strong>Tapered Body Mechanics:</strong> Two serrated insulating plastic wedges slide along internal tapered tracks within the clamp body. As wind, ice, or cable weight pulls the cable away, the wedges are drawn deeper into the taper, increasing radial clamping force proportionally with line tension.</li><li><strong>Uniform Load Distribution:</strong> The elongated wedges distribute mechanical clamping pressure over a wide surface area of the cable jacket. The grooved plastic teeth grip the outer XLPE sheath firmly to prevent slippage while maintaining the full dielectric integrity of the cable insulation without crushing or piercing.</li><li><strong>Tool-Free Installation:</strong> Linemen slide the wedges apart, insert the bundled cable, pull the cable snug by hand, and engage the bail hook onto the bracket. The line self-tightens automatically as soon as cable tension is released.</li></ul>

What is the difference between a Dead End Clamp and an Intermediate Suspension Clamp?

While both fittings are essential line hardware for overhead aerial bundled cables, they serve opposite mechanical purposes: <ul><li><strong>Dead End Clamp (Anchoring):</strong> Carries full line mechanical tension (up to 3.5kN). It rigidly anchors the cable at line ends, facades, and sharp turns, terminating the span tension so that forces are not transmitted downstream.</li><li><strong>Suspension Clamp (Intermediate):</strong> Carries only the vertical dead weight of the cable span along straight-line utility poles. The cable passes freely through a hinged thermoplastic sleeve, allowing axial movement and longitudinal oscillation during wind and thermal expansion.</li></ul>
