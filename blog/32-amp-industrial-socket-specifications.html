32 A Industrial Socket: Specifications, Pin Configuration and Cable Sizing

ET
ET Engineering Team October 24, 2026 10 min read

A 32 A industrial socket is not defined by its current rating alone. Four things have to agree before the assembly works: the rated current, the pin configuration, the voltage and frequency code expressed as colour plus clock position, and the ingress protection rating. Get one of them wrong and the outcome is not a slightly suboptimal installation — it is a plug that physically will not enter the socket, or an assembly that fails inspection on the day it is signed off.

The reason this trips people up is that a 32 A industrial connector looks interchangeable across a catalogue page. Blue and red, three, four or five pins, all roughly the same size. The differences are in the keyway, the sleeve diameter and the marking, and none of them are visible at a glance in a product photograph.

What "32 A" actually fixes

The rating fixes the contact system, the housing and the cable gland range. It does not fix the circuit. Cable size and protective device are chosen separately, from the installation method and the load, and a 32 A socket can legitimately sit on a circuit protected at a lower rating if the cable is small or the run is long.

What the rating does enforce is mechanical exclusion. In the IEC 60309 pin-and-sleeve family, the current rating is part of the keying, so a 16 A plug cannot be inserted into a 32 A socket and a 63 A plug cannot be forced into either. That exclusion is the safety feature: it stops a 16 A extension lead being plugged into a circuit that can deliver far more.

Colour and clock code

Industrial connectors encode their intended voltage band in the housing colour, and the exact voltage and frequency in the clock position of the earth pin. Both must match between plug and socket; a matching colour with the wrong clock position is still a mismatch.

ColourVoltage bandTypical use
Yellow100–130 VSite tools and temporary lighting on 110 V reduced-low-voltage systems
Blue200–250 VSingle-phase tools, caravans, small plant, 230 V site power
Red380–480 VThree-phase distribution, 400 V motors and plant

When you order, quote the colour and the number of pins together. "32 A red five-pin" is an unambiguous instruction; "32 A three-phase" is not, because it does not say whether a neutral is required.

Pin configurations

ConfigurationPinsWhat it carriesWhere it belongs
2P+E3Live, neutral, earthSingle-phase tools and 230 V plant
3P+E4Three lives plus earth, no neutralThree-phase motors and balanced plant loads
3P+N+E5Three lives, neutral and earthThree-phase distribution boards and mixed 230/400 V loads

The four-pin versus five-pin decision is the one most often made too late. A four-pin outlet cannot supply a 230 V load from a three-phase feed, because there is no neutral to return it. If the assembly will ever feed a distribution board with single-phase final circuits, specify five-pin from the start — converting later means changing the outlet, the plug and the cable.

IP44 is the common indoor default and is often adequate. Cable sizing for a 32 A radial

The socket rating tells you what the connector can carry; the cable has to carry it in the conditions it is actually installed in. As a working starting point for copper conductors on a 32 A single-phase radial, 6 mm² covers most clipping and trunking methods, and 10 mm² is the usual step up where the cable is grouped with others, run through thermal insulation, or in a high ambient.

Treat that as a starting point, not a specification. Current-carrying capacity depends on installation method, ambient temperature, grouping and the number of loaded conductors, and every wiring regulation applies correction factors to a base table. Confirm the final size against the regulation in force on the project and the manufacturer's data for the cable actually being used.

Two practical points matter more than the table. First, the gland range of the socket must accept the cable you have chosen — a 10 mm² flexible cord will not pass a gland sized for 6 mm². Second, keep the run as short as the layout allows; voltage drop on a long 32 A radial is usually what forces the next size up, not thermal capacity.

Breaker coordination

The protective device protects the cable. It is not there to match the socket label. In practice that means the breaker must not exceed the corrected current-carrying capacity of the conductor, and the circuit usually carries additional protection by RCD for socket outlets in accordance with the local wiring regulation.

Where a single enclosure carries several outlets, coordination becomes a space question as much as an electrical one. The YM-012 is a 380×280×155 mm IP65 enclosure built around a breaker window plus outlet positions, which is the configuration that lets a 32 A feed and its final circuits live in one box instead of two.

Pre-order checklist

  • Rated current confirmed as 32 A, not inferred from the plug that happens to be on the tool.
  • Pin configuration stated: 3-pin, 4-pin or 5-pin, and whether a neutral is required.
  • Colour and clock position quoted, so the mating plug is guaranteed to fit.
  • IP rating matched to the mounting position, with the lid seal rated to the same figure.
  • Cable size and gland range checked against each other before the enclosure is specified.
  • Number of breaker ways and outlet positions agreed, including spare capacity.
  • Whether the assembly is a standard configuration or built to a drawing.

The last item is where most custom projects actually differ. A stock IP65 box with a fixed outlet mix covers the common site case; anything with a specific breaker count, a mixed 16 A and 32 A outlet pattern, or a particular cable entry arrangement is a drawing-based build, and it is worth saying so on the enquiry rather than after the order.

Frequently asked questions

Can a 16 A plug be inserted into a 32 A socket?

No. In the IEC 60309 pin-and-sleeve system the rating is part of the keying, so the sleeve diameter and pin spacing of a 32 A outlet will not accept a 16 A plug. That is deliberate: it prevents a 16 A appliance being connected to a circuit protected at a higher rating.

Is IP65 enough for an outdoor socket?

IP65 means dust-tight and protected against water jets, which covers most wall-mounted outdoor positions under a soffit or inside a plant enclosure. Where the assembly will be hosed down or can stand in water, specify IP67 and check that the lid seal is rated for the same figure as the enclosure.

Does a 32 A socket need a 32 A breaker?

The protective device is chosen for the cable, not for the socket, so the breaker must not exceed the cable current-carrying capacity. In practice a 32 A radial feed is protected at 32 A with a 30 mA RCD where the installation rules require additional protection for socket outlets; confirm the exact requirement against the local wiring regulation before ordering.
