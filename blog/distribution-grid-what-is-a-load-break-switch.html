Medium-Voltage Distribution & Load Break Switches / Medium-Voltage Switchgear

Distribution Grid Switching: What Is a Load Break Switch?

ET
ET Engineering Team October 18, 2026 10 min read

The Critical Sectionalizing and Switching Foundation for Modern Medium-Voltage Grids

Inside electrical distribution networks, compact secondary substations, renewable solar/wind gathering stations, and industrial manufacturing plants operating at 11kV, 24kV, or 33kV/36kV, electrical engineers face continuous operational switching requirements.

Feeder lines must be sectionalized for routine maintenance, transformer branches must be isolated without interrupting upstream substations, and ring distribution topologies must be reconfigured to maintain power continuity during local grid disturbances.

However, installing full-scale vacuum or SF6 circuit breakers at every switching point is economically prohibitive, while standard disconnectors (isolators) cannot be operated under load without triggering catastrophic electrical explosions.

The Medium-Voltage Load Break Switch (Model FN18 / FLN36 / FKN12 Series) provides the optimal technical and economic balance: a rugged, high-speed switching apparatus capable of safely interrupting full rated load currents (630A) and making onto short circuits, complete with integrated earthing safety and modular high-voltage current-limiting fuse protection.

Engineering Anatomy: Inside a Model FLN36 / FKN12 Medium-Voltage Load Break Switch

  1. Rotary Puffing Arc-Quenching Chamber: Utilizes SF6 gas insulation or vacuum interruption chambers. As contacts separate, an integrated piston compresses gas into a high-velocity blast directed across the contact gap, cooling and de-ionizing the AC plasma arc at the first natural zero-crossing.
  2. Stored Energy Spring-Operating Mechanism: Operates independently of the operator's handle speed. A charged spring snaps the contacts open or closed in milliseconds, ensuring consistent arc interruption and preventing contact burning.
  3. Mechanically Interlocked Earth Switch: Integrates high-conductivity earthing blades capable of withstanding short-circuit making currents (50kA peak), mechanically interlocked with the main contacts to ensure 100% fail-safe maintenance grounding.
  4. Modular High-Voltage Fuse-Combination Base: Heavy-duty silver-plated spring clips accommodate high-rupturing-capacity (HRC) current-limiting fuses. Features a mechanical trip linkage that automatically trips the LBS open if any phase fuse blows.
  5. Robust Epoxy Resin Insulating Enclosure: Cast from high-grade cycloaliphatic epoxy resin, providing superior mechanical rigidity, high dielectric strength, and high creepage distances that resist tracking in humid and contaminated substation environments.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a Load Break Switch (LBS) and how does it differ from a Circuit Breaker and an Isolator?

A Load Break Switch (LBS)—frequently designated in utility distribution engineering as an interrupter switch, switch-disconnector, or sectionalizer switch—is a medium-voltage mechanical switching device designed to <strong>safely make, carry, and interrupt normal operational load currents</strong> (typically 630A at 12kV, 24kV, or 36kV), as well as closed-loop feeder loop currents and transformer magnetizing currents. In modern electrical distribution networks: <ul><li><strong>Difference from an Isolator (Disconnector):</strong> An isolator has zero arc-extinguishing capability and can ONLY be opened when the circuit is completely de-energized under zero load. Opening an isolator under live load draws a continuous plasma arc exceeding 3000°C, resulting in explosive phase-to-phase flashover. An LBS incorporates high-speed spring mechanisms and an arc-quenching chamber that quenches full load arcs in milliseconds.</li><li><strong>Difference from a Circuit Breaker (VCB/SF6):</strong> A circuit breaker is engineered to interrupt massive, symmetrical short-circuit fault currents (25kA to 40kA) via complex protective relay trip units. A circuit breaker costs 3× to 5× more than a load break switch. An LBS handles daily switching and sectionalizing, relying on downstream current-limiting fuses to clear short-circuits.</li></ul>

How does a Load Break Switch and High-Voltage Fuse Combination protect distribution transformers?

Under standard IEC 62271-105 specifications, a Load Break Switch is frequently paired directly with three high-voltage current-limiting fuse-links: <ul><li><strong>Operational Switching:</strong> The LBS handles routine operational switching: connecting, disconnecting, and sectionalizing the transformer under full rated load (e.g. 500kVA to 2500kVA distribution transformers).</li><li><strong>Short-Circuit Clearance:</strong> If an internal transformer short-circuit or phase-to-phase cable fault occurs, the high-voltage current-limiting fuses melt and clear the fault current in under 5 to 10 milliseconds, cutting off peak short-circuit currents long before they reach destructive levels. When any single fuse blows, an integrated spring-loaded striker pin triggers the LBS mechanical trip latch, automatically opening all three phases simultaneously to prevent single-phasing damage.</li></ul>

What is the function of the Integrated Earth Switch in an LBS cubicle?

Safety interlocking is mandatory in medium-voltage switchgear. YOMIN LBS units incorporate an integrated, mechanically interlocked earth switch: <ul><li><strong>Fail-Safe Mechanical Interlock:</strong> The main switch contacts and the earth switch contacts share a mechanical key or lever interlock that makes it physically impossible to close the earth switch while the main load break switch is closed.</li><li><strong>Personnel Maintenance Safety:</strong> Before utility technicians open the cable compartment for cable testing or maintenance, the main switch is opened, and the earth switch is closed, safely discharging all capacitive residual charges from underground cables directly to the station earth grid.</li></ul>
