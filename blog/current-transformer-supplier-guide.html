How to Choose a Current Transformer Supplier: A Procurement Guide

ET Engineering Team 9 min read · August 31, 2026

A current transformer (CT) is the component that steps the primary current of a feeder down to a safe, measurable secondary value (typically 5 A or 1 A) for meters, relays and instruments. Its accuracy class literally decides how much you are billed — or how correctly your protection operates.

If you are a panel builder, meter distributor or OEM importing CTs, the supplier you choose determines whether the ratio is accurate, the class is real, and the batch is consistent. This guide is a procurement checklist: what to verify, what to test, and which supplier claims to treat with suspicion.

Why Supplier Quality Matters

A CT is a magnetic component: its accuracy depends on core material, winding quality and manufacturing consistency. Two visually identical CTs can differ in actual accuracy class. When CTs feed billing meters, a Class 0.5 CT that is really Class 1.0 under-serves the meter and quietly shifts billing error. When they feed protection relays, a wrong ratio or saturation point can mean a fault is not seen.

Key Specifications to Verify

1. Accuracy class

Class 0.5S/0.5 for billing-grade metering, Class 1.0 for general monitoring, Class 5P/10P for protection. The class must be guaranteed with a test report, not just printed on the label. Read our guide to ratio, class and burden selection for the technical detail.

2. Ratio

Standard ratios run 100/5 A up to 5,000/5 A (and higher for busbar types). Confirm the ratio matches the feeder's expected full load and that the CT can carry the fault current without saturating prematurely.

3. Burden (VA)

The CT's rated burden (e.g. 2.5 VA, 5 VA, 10 VA) must cover the connected meter/instrument load plus wiring. Undersized burden is one of the most common field errors — the meter reads low because the CT saturates.

4. Window size and mounting

Split-core CTs clamp around existing cables without disconnection — ideal for retrofits and sub-metering. Solid/ring types are lower cost and higher accuracy for new installations. Busbar types mount directly on panel busbars. Choose the form factor that fits the installation: see our CT selection guide for details.

5. Insulation and voltage class

Low-voltage CTs are typically rated 660–720 V; higher voltage classes exist for busbar and outdoor use. Confirm the insulation class matches your system.

What to Ask Every Supplier

  1. Factory or trader? — Ask for production line photos/video and the factory's address. A genuine manufacturer shows winding, core and test stations.
  2. Test evidence — Request the type test certificate (ratio error, phase error, temperature rise, insulation) from an accredited lab, and batch test records.
  3. Core material — silicon steel grain-oriented cores give stable accuracy; ask what grade is used and how saturation is controlled.
  4. Custom ratios — OEM buyers need non-standard ratios and secondary currents (1 A, 0.333 V output for energy harvesting CTs). Confirm the supplier winds custom values.
  5. QC process — 100% testing vs sampling; what is tested on every unit (ratio, polarity, burden, insulation)?
  6. Certifications — CE, RoHS, and IEC compliance documentation.
  7. Lead time and batch consistency — can they hold tolerance across large batches?

Red Flags

  • Class promised but no test report available.
  • No factory address or only a trading-office address on the invoice.
  • Can't explain burden or don't know what secondary current their CT provides.
  • One "universal" CT sold for every ratio with just a label change.
  • Pricing far below market for the same class — accuracy costs money.

Testing Your Own Samples

Before a bulk order, bench-test samples: ratio and polarity with a CT analyzer or dual-meter method, burden performance with the rated VA connected, and a visual check of core and winding. A 30-minute test beats a year of wrong billing.

FAQ

What accuracy class do I need for billing?

Class 0.5S or 0.5 for revenue metering; Class 1.0 for monitoring. Protection uses 5P/10P classes with different error limits. Confirm with the meter's own class and your utility's requirements.

Split-core or solid CT?

Split-core for retrofits and sub-metering where cables cannot be disconnected; solid/ring type for new panels where accuracy and cost matter most. Accuracy of a well-made solid CT is easier to hold.

Can a supplier make custom ratios?

A real manufacturer can — winding custom ratios and secondary values (5 A, 1 A, 0.333 V) is routine for OEM programs. A trader will only resell standard values.

How do I know the CT's class is real?

Demand the type test certificate and batch records, then test samples yourself. If the label and the report disagree, that's the answer.

Conclusion

Buying current transformers is buying accuracy — verify the class with evidence, match ratio and burden to the installation, and choose a manufacturer that winds, tests and documents its own product.
