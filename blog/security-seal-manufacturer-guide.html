Security Seal Manufacturer: What to Look for When Sourcing Meter Seals

ET Engineering Team 8 min read · August 31, 2026

A security seal is a tamper-evident device that shows whether an enclosure — an electricity meter, a container, a valve, a cash box — has been opened. For utilities, a seal is not decoration: it is the physical evidence layer behind revenue protection and safety inspection programs.

If you are a utility, meter distributor, customs authority or logistics company sourcing seals in bulk, the manufacturer decision determines whether your seals actually deter tampering, carry your branding, and pass audits. This guide covers seal types, the performance characteristics that matter, and the specific checks to run on any potential supplier.

Why Seals Matter in Metering

Prepaid and postpaid metering programs lose revenue to meter bypass and tampering. A numbered, branded seal creates three things:

  • Deterrence — a visible, difficult-to-replicate seal discourages casual tampering.
  • Evidence — a broken or missing seal is proof of access, supporting disconnection or penalty decisions.
  • Audit trail — unique serial numbers tie each meter to its last inspection, technician and date.

Types of Security Seals

TypeHow it worksTypical use
Wire / twist-tite sealStainless or galvanised wire locked in a one-way body; the classic "meter seal"Electricity and water meters, test terminals
Bolt sealSteel bolt locked into a body; very high pull strengthContainers, trucks, large valves, high-security assets
Cable sealFlexible steel cable with locking bodyBulk containers, tanker valves, equipment
Padlock sealOne-time plastic body that cannot be re-lockedCabinets, boxes, meter boards
Plastic pull-up / strap sealStrap pulled through a ratchet bodyBags, smaller boxes, retail tamper evidence

For meter programs specifically, the wire seal is the workhorse — it threads through the meter's terminal cover and test terminal block, and cannot be removed without breaking.

What to Check in a Security Seal

1. Tamper evidence and one-way design

The body must lock irreversibly. Test that the wire or bolt cannot be withdrawn without visible damage, and that the seal cannot be re-assembled after opening.

2. Break strength

Ask for the minimum pull strength (e.g. 20–50 kg for wire seals; 1,000+ kg for bolt seals). The value should be documented, not promised verbally.

3. Materials and environment

Seals sit outdoors on meters for years: the wire should be stainless or galvanised steel, the body UV-stable ABS or polycarbonate, and printing must survive weather, not fade or flake.

4. Custom branding and numbering

Most utilities seal with their own logo, name and sequential numbers (laser or inkjet printed). Confirm the supplier offers custom moulding or printing, minimum order sizes for custom runs, and that numbering is consecutive and controlled.

5. Batch QC and traceability

A serious manufacturer tests samples from every batch for break strength and locking integrity, and can issue a batch certificate with test values.

Supplier Checklist for Bulk Seals

  1. Request a physical sample kit and test it: lock, pull strength, removal without damage.
  2. Verify custom logo and serial-number capability, and the MOQ for your design.
  3. Ask for a batch test certificate and the defect-rate commitment.
  4. Confirm material specs (wire grade, body plastic, UV and temperature range).
  5. Check delivery lead time and whether packaging supports field dispensing (boxes, bags, dispenser packs).
  6. Ask whether the manufacturer also supplies related meter accessories — a single supplier for meters, seals and test blocks simplifies procurement.

FAQ

What is the difference between a seal and a lock?

A lock is reusable; a seal is one-time. A seal records that access happened; a lock only delays it. Metering programs use seals because the audit trail matters more than the delay.

Can seals be removed and re-applied without detection?

A well-designed one-way seal cannot. The body is destroyed or visibly damaged on removal, and serial numbers are tied to inspection records — which is exactly why numbered seals are used.

What pull strength do meter seals need?

Typical wire meter seals are specified at roughly 20–50 kg minimum pull strength — enough to resist casual removal while remaining field-serviceable with the right cutter. Confirm the documented value with the supplier.
