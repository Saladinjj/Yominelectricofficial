Screw Machine Manufacturer: How to Qualify, Sample and Order

ET
ET Engineering Team November 23, 2026 10 min read

A screw machine manufacturer for the electrical industry is not the same business as a screw-machine shop that turns fasteners on a lathe. If you are buying equipment that drills, taps and screws brass busbar rows and aluminium terminals together, you are sourcing an integrated line — and the questions that decide whether it works are about repeatable hole position, repeatable torque and cycle time, not about spindle speed. Most failed purchases in this category come from a supplier quoting a machine it has never built for that material, or from a buyer sending a photograph instead of a drawing.

Two different businesses answer to the same search term

“Screw machine” carries two meanings in English, and they lead to completely different factories.

  • The machining sense. An automatic screw machine is a lathe-type turning centre that makes small threaded parts from bar stock. Its output is a component.
  • The assembly sense. A screw tightening machine drives screws into a pre-drilled part. Its output is an assembled busbar row or terminal block.

YOMIN builds the second kind, and the line goes further than tightening: it starts with the deep processing of the bar (cutting, drilling, tapping, chamfering) and ends with a finishing pass. That distinction matters at the quotation stage. A lathe builder will quote you a machine that produces screws; you need one that consumes them.

What an integrated line actually contains

The configuration below is the one YOMIN manufactures: an integrated metal deep processing line with three production stages, working brass and aluminium busbar rows and terminals, with a touch screen and PLC control at each station.

StageWhat the station doesWhat to verify before you sign
1. Deep processing Automatic compound machining centre that cuts, drills, taps and chamfers brass and aluminium busbar rows. Horizontal, PLC-controlled with touch screen. Hole position tolerance across a full bar, not one sample hole. Whether different hole sizes, aluminium profiles and double-row hole patterns are genuinely customisable or only offered within a fixed window.
2. Screw tightening Vertical automatic screwing station that integrates screwing and testing, fed by conveyor with pre-drilled busbars. Torque repeatability over a shift, not a single reading. Whether the screw-driving layout can be re-laid for your pattern, and how a cross-thread or missing screw is detected.
3. Rotary finishing Circulating rotary metalworking machine with a turntable flow structure, cutting, tapping, drilling and chamfering semi-finished copper and aluminium parts. Turntable index accuracy and how the station handles parts that arrive out of tolerance from stage 1.

Read that table as a buying document rather than a specification. Each right-hand column is a question that a serious manufacturer can answer with a drawing, a test record or a video of your own part running — and a trading company cannot.

What to send before anyone can quote

Quotations for this equipment diverge wildly because buyers send different amounts of information. A supplier who receives a photograph will quote a standard machine and hope it fits. Send the following and the quotes become comparable:

  • The busbar or terminal drawing, dimensioned, with the hole pattern and the thread callout for every hole.
  • Material and temper — brass and aluminium machine very differently, and aluminium profiles behave differently again from flat bar.
  • Bar envelope: length, width, thickness, and the longest part the line must handle.
  • Output target, stated as parts per hour at a realistic efficiency, plus the number of shifts per week.
  • The screw: thread size, length, head type, drive type and the torque window with its tolerance.
  • Acceptance expectations: which dimensions you will measure, on how many pieces, and what you will reject on.
  • Site constraints: supply voltage, available floor space, compressed air, and how the line will be loaded and unloaded.

If a supplier quotes without the drawing and the screw specification, the number is not a quote — it is an indication, and it will move.

How to qualify the manufacturer, not just the machine

The equipment is assembled from bought-in motion components, so almost any supplier can produce a plausible brochure. What separates a manufacturer from a reseller is what happens between your drawing and the crate:

  • Ask who machines the fixture and the tooling. Custom screw-driving layouts and drilled guide plates are made, not catalogued. If everything is outsourced, your change order has two lead times in series.
  • Ask which PLC platform and who writes the program. YOMIN uses a touch screen with a PLC per station. Confirm the platform is one your maintenance team can support, and that you receive the program, not just a locked panel.
  • Ask for a run on your part, or the closest part they have. A video of a similar busbar is acceptable evidence; a video of a different product category is not.
  • Ask what is customised and what is fixed. YOMIN customises to drawings or samples; get the boundary in writing so a later change is a variation, not a surprise.
  • Ask for the wear parts list with each item's life. Screwdriver bits, guide bushes and tap holders are consumables. A supplier who cannot list them has not maintained this kind of line.
  • Ask how the machine is tested before shipment, and whether you will receive the record.

Where the money actually goes

Three configurations get quoted for the same job, and they are not three prices for one machine — they are three different operating models. Compare them on total cost per thousand parts, not on purchase price.

ConfigurationWhat you are buyingWhere it winsWhere it hurts
Manual cell Drill press, tapping head and hand screwdrivers Low capital, fast to start, easy to change Torque depends on the operator; scrap and rework appear as a hidden cost on every batch
Semi-automatic Powered stations with manual transfer between them Good torque control at a moderate capital cost Throughput is capped by the transfer; the weakest station sets the rate
Integrated line Deep processing, screw tightening and rotary finishing in one flow, PLC per station Repeatability and labour per shift; one operator supervises rather than performs Changeover discipline is mandatory; a poorly specified first order is expensive to correct

The integrated line is the right answer when the part is stable and the volume is real. It is the wrong answer when the drawing is still changing every month, because every change becomes a fixture change.

Acceptance test on arrival

The machine will run on the day it is installed. The question is whether it still holds tolerance in week six. Run this test before the balance payment and before you dismantle the packing:

  • Hole position at the two ends and the middle of a full-length bar, measured on the same datum the drawing uses.
  • Torque on the first, the hundredth and the thousandth screw, with a calibrated driver, recorded as three numbers rather than one.
  • Cycle time for a full bar, timed, at the efficiency you were quoted.
  • Fault behaviour: deliberately feed a cross-threaded screw and a missing screw, and confirm the station stops rather than passing the part.
  • Burr and chamfer at the cut ends, checked visually against the sample you approved.
  • Drawings and program handed over, including the I/O list and the parameter backup.

Frequently asked questions

Is a screw machine manufacturer the same as a lathe manufacturer?

No. A lathe-type automatic screw machine produces threaded parts from bar stock; a screw tightening machine assembles pre-drilled parts by driving screws. YOMIN builds integrated metal deep processing lines for brass and aluminium busbar rows and terminals, which start with cutting, drilling, tapping and chamfering and end with screw tightening and rotary finishing.

What do I need to send to get a usable quotation?

A dimensioned drawing with the hole pattern and thread callout, the material and temper, the bar envelope, the output target in parts per hour, the screw specification with its torque window, and your acceptance criteria. Without the drawing and the screw specification the returned figure is an indication rather than a quote.

Can the line be built for aluminium as well as brass?

Yes. YOMIN specifies the line for brass and aluminium busbar rows and terminals, and both materials run through the same three stages. Aluminium needs different cutting parameters and chip control, so state the alloy and temper in the enquiry rather than saying only “aluminium”.

What is customised and what is standard?

The line is customised to drawings or samples: hole sizes, aluminium profiles, double-row hole patterns and screw-driving layouts can be built to your part. The control architecture — touch screen and PLC at each station — is the standard platform. YOMIN quotes each configuration individually, so the price is confirmed against your drawing rather than published as a list figure.

Sourcing a screw machine or a full processing line?

Send your busbar drawing, material and hole pattern. We build integrated metal deep processing lines for brass and aluminium busbar rows and terminals, customised to your drawings or samples.
