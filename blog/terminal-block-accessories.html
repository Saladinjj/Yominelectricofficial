Terminal Block Accessories: What to Order With Every DIN Rail Mount Terminal Block

ET
ET Engineering Team November 21, 2026 8 min read

Short answer: a terminal row is not finished when the blocks arrive. Every DIN rail mount terminal block needs accessories to stay put, stay insulated and stay traceable: an end bracket at each end of the row, an end plate wherever a section ends, a jumper bar or comb busbar wherever several terminals share one potential, partition plates between different circuits, and a marking strip on every terminal. Order them with the blocks, in the same family, or the panel gets assembled twice.

This is the part of the order that most often goes missing. Blocks are counted carefully because they appear on the schematic; accessories are not drawn, so they are not counted, so they are not bought. The result is a row that slides on the rail, an exposed live end, and an electrician bridging terminals with loops of wire because the comb bar never arrived.

What counts as a terminal block accessory

An accessory is anything that makes the row work as an assembly rather than as a pile of individual blocks. That covers mechanical parts (end brackets, end plates, partition plates), electrical parts (jumper bars, comb busbars, test terminals, earth terminals) and identification (marking strips, markers).

Two things are worth stating plainly, because they drive both cost and lead time. First, accessories are family-specific: a jumper bar from one manufacturer usually will not seat in another manufacturer's blocks, even when both claim the same 35 mm rail and the same pitch. Second, accessories are cheap per piece and expensive per omission - a missing end plate costs a few cents and can hold up an inspection.

The accessories a DIN rail mount terminal block row needs

Six items cover almost every panel. The quantity rules below are the ones that survive contact with a real assembly bench.

The row above is also a useful RFQ template. Send it with quantities and the accessory list stops being an afterthought on both sides of the order.

Jumper bars and comb busbars carry current, so they need a rating

This is the accessory where a wrong choice becomes a thermal problem rather than a cosmetic one. A jumper bar is a current-carrying part, and the current it can carry is not the same as the current the block can carry. The group is limited by the weakest element in the chain: the bar, the contact between bar and block, or the incoming feed.

Insulated comb busbarWire jumper / daisy chain
Current pathSolid copper comb with fork or pin contactsConductor looped through each terminal clamp
Rating comes fromThe bar itself, stated as a continuous currentThe wire size and each individual termination
AssemblyPress-on, one operation for the whole groupOne termination per terminal, plus dressing
InspectionSingle rigid part, visible at a glanceLoops can be left loose or over-bent
Best used forFeeds, neutral and earth distribution across many terminalsTwo or three terminals, or where a comb will not fit

Two rules keep this simple. Match the bar's continuous rating to the feed, not to the sum of the block ratings, and never bridge terminals that sit on different potentials just because the pitch lines up.

End plates, partition plates and touch protection

End plates and partition plates are often treated as the same part. They are not. An end plate closes the outer face of the last terminal in a section, so no live part is reachable from the side. A partition plate sits between two terminals inside the row and increases the distance between different potentials.

Both matter more in higher-voltage panels and in anything that will be inspected against a clearance table. They are also the two accessories most often left off a purchase order, because a drawing shows terminals in a row and says nothing about what closes the ends.

Marking is the cheapest hour you will ever save

A marking strip costs a fraction of a terminal and saves an electrician an hour on the first fault. Two systems are common: a strip that clips onto the block and carries a printed legend, and individual markers that snap onto the conductor or the terminal. Choose one system for the whole panel; mixing them is how a drawing stops matching the hardware.

Whatever the system, the marking must be generated from the same terminal schedule used to buy the blocks. If the schedule and the markers are produced separately, they will disagree.

Keep one family on one rail

Blocks from different manufacturers will usually clip onto the same TS35 rail and will usually accept the same conductor. What does not transfer is the accessory interface: the shape of the jumper bar channel, the way an end plate engages, the position of the marking groove.

The practical rule is one family per rail section. On a retrofit where two families must coexist, put the boundary at a physical break - a partition plate and a separate end bracket - rather than mixing accessories inside one group.

Frequently asked questions

Do terminal block accessories have to be the same brand as the blocks?

Mechanically they will often clip on, but jumper bars, end plates and partition plates are usually not interchangeable between manufacturers. Keep one family per rail section and put any unavoidable boundary at a physical break.

How many end brackets does a row need?

At minimum one at each end. Add intermediate stops on long rows and on either side of heavy devices, because a long unsupported row can creep along the rail under vibration.

Can I replace jumper bars with a comb busbar?

Yes, when every bridged terminal shares the same potential and the bar's continuous rating covers the feed. Do not use a comb to bridge different potentials, and do not size it from the sum of the block ratings.

Do I still need end plates if the row ends against an enclosure wall?

Yes. A wall does not close the live side of the terminal or maintain the insulation distance, and it will not satisfy an inspector looking for a closed end.

What should an accessory RFQ contain?

The block family and module width, conductor range, quantity per circuit, the accessory quantities from the checklist above, and the marking system. Add the rail profile if the row is being assembled into an existing panel.

Building a terminal row for a panel?

Send the terminal schedule and the panel drawing. We will match the blocks, comb busbars, end plates and marking to one family before the order is placed.
