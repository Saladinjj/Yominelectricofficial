Smart Metering & AMI: What Is a LoRaWAN Energy Meter?

ET
ET Engineering Team October 13, 2026 10 min read

The Private Wireless Revolution in Modern Utility Energy Metering

Across national electrical power utilities, municipal distribution grids, multi-tenant commercial towers, and smart industrial campuses worldwide, electricity providers are undergoing a massive transition from manual meter reading to automated Advanced Metering Infrastructure (AMI).

Historically, automated smart meters relied on public cellular networks (2G, 3G, 4G, and NB-IoT). While cellular communication delivers data, it imposes a punishing financial burden on utilities: every meter requires a dedicated telco SIM card, forcing utilities to pay recurring monthly carrier subscription fees for decades.

Furthermore, cellular coverage is notoriously unreliable in underground basement switchrooms, metal electrical metering cabinets, and remote rural microgrids.

The LoRaWAN Long-Range Wireless Smart Energy Meter (Model DTS / DDS Series) delivers the definitive technological breakthrough: a high-precision smart electricity meter utilizing unlicensed sub-gigahertz radio waves to transmit real-time consumption data over 5 to 15 kilometers directly to utility-owned gateways—achieving 100% automated meter reading with zero carrier SIM subscription fees.

Engineering Anatomy: Inside a Model DTS LoRaWAN Smart Electricity Meter

  1. Sub-GHz LoRaWAN Transceiver Node: Integrated industrial radio module operating on regional license-free ISM bands (EU868, US915, AS923, RU864) with Chirp Spread Spectrum modulation, delivering link budgets up to 157 dB.
  2. High-Precision Metrology Processing Engine: Dedicated 24-bit dual ADC metering chip measuring active energy (kWh), reactive energy (kvarh), apparent power (kVA), voltage, current, power factor, and frequency with Class 1.0 or Class 0.5S accuracy.
  3. Bi-Stable Internal Latching Relay (60A to 100A): High-capacity magnetic latching contactor allows utility operators to execute remote over-the-air (OTA) connect and disconnect commands, overload tripping, and prepayment control.
  4. Standardized DLMS/COSEM Protocol Engine: Embeds international utility data exchange standards (IEC 62056) over LoRaWAN payloads, ensuring seamless plug-and-play interoperability with any commercial AMI Head-End System (HES) or MDM software.
  5. Anti-Tamper Physical and Magnetic Sensors: Multiple tamper detection circuits record terminal cover opening, reverse current flow, neutral bypass, and strong external magnetic interference, transmitting instant alarm packets to the utility.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a LoRaWAN Energy Meter and how does it revolutionize utility Advanced Metering Infrastructure (AMI)?

A LoRaWAN Energy Meter—frequently designated as a long-range wireless smart electricity meter, sub-GHz IoT energy meter, or wireless AMR meter—is a precision digital electricity meter integrated with an embedded sub-gigahertz LoRaWAN wireless transceiver. In modern utility electrical distribution networks, commercial shopping centers, industrial campuses, and residential housing complexes: <ul><li><strong>The Cellular &amp; Manual Meter Reading Problem:</strong> Traditional utility billing relies either on manual meter readers (prone to human errors, estimated bills, and high labor costs) or cellular 4G/GPRS smart meters. While cellular meters transmit data, every single meter requires an active cellular SIM card. For a utility deploying 100,000 meters, monthly SIM data subscription carrier fees cost millions of dollars annually. Furthermore, cellular signals fail to penetrate underground basement electrical vaults and thick concrete walls.</li><li><strong>The LoRaWAN Solution:</strong> LoRaWAN operates on <strong>unlicensed sub-GHz industrial radio spectrum</strong> (such as 868 MHz in Europe or 915 MHz in the Americas) utilizing Chirp Spread Spectrum (CSS) modulation. A single central utility LoRaWAN gateway mounted on a tower or rooftop connects thousands of smart meters across a <strong>5 to 15 kilometer radius</strong>. The utility owns the private wireless network infrastructure with <strong>ZERO monthly cellular carrier SIM fees</strong>, receiving automated hourly kilowatt-hour readings, peak demand data, power quality telemetry, and tamper alerts directly into their head-end system (HES).</li></ul>

How does LoRaWAN achieve deep signal penetration through concrete basements and underground vaults?

Conventional wireless technologies like Wi-Fi (2.4 GHz) and Cellular 4G (1.8–2.6 GHz) operate at high frequencies with short wavelengths that suffer severe attenuation when passing through solid materials. LoRaWAN utilizes low-frequency sub-gigahertz radio waves (868 MHz / 915 MHz) combined with Chirp Spread Spectrum (CSS) modulation: <ul><li><strong>Superior Obstacle Diffraction:</strong> Sub-GHz radio waves bend around physical obstacles and penetrate multiple levels of reinforced concrete floors, metal electrical enclosures, and underground utility vaults where cellular signals show "No Service".</li><li><strong>Extreme Receiver Sensitivity (Down to -137 dBm):</strong> CSS spreads the signal across a wider bandwidth, allowing LoRaWAN gateways to decode meter data transmissions that are up to 20 dB below the electrical thermal noise floor, guaranteeing 99.5%+ packet delivery rates.</li></ul>

Can a LoRaWAN smart meter perform remote revenue disconnection and prepayment control?

Yes. YOMIN LoRaWAN Smart Meters can be equipped with an integrated high-capacity <strong>bi-stable magnetic latching relay</strong> (up to 100A). Operating as a LoRaWAN Class C device (continuously listening for downlink commands) or Class A device (receiving downlinks during receive windows after uplink), utility billing operators can issue remote over-the-air (OTA) disconnect commands if a consumer fails to pay, or automatically reconnect service instantly upon mobile bill payment—eliminating the need to dispatch technicians to the property.
