## 1 — Grounding Busbar Guide: Low-Impedance Panel Earthing & Fault Clearance

**HOOK**
A 25kA ground fault inside a low-voltage switchboard lasts only 0.5 seconds before the circuit breaker clears it.

In those 500 milliseconds, an undersized or corroded grounding busbar will vaporize into molten copper shrapnel, destroying the entire lineup.

**PROBLEM**
Inadequate earthing busbar sizing and galvanic corrosion in industrial power distribution panels.

Electrical contractors frequently treat ground bars as passive metal strips rather than active fault-clearing conductors. Worse, terminating aluminium grounding conductors directly onto bare copper bars creates aggressive bimetallic oxidation, producing high contact resistance that raises chassis touch voltages to lethal levels during earth faults.

**SOLUTION**
Engineered **YM-GB Series Solid Electrolytic Copper & Tin-Plated Aluminium Grounding Busbar Systems**.

Three core electrical design rules:
&bull; **ETP 99.9% pure copper (100% IACS):** Delivers lowest possible contact impedance, guaranteeing instantaneous clearing of line-to-ground faults within statutory touch voltage limits ($< 50\text{V}$)
&bull; **Heavy electro-tin plating (8–12 &mu;m):** Eliminates bimetallic galvanic potential difference, allowing dual-rated AL9CU mechanical lugs and aluminium wires to bolt directly without pitting or overheating
&bull; **High-temperature standoff isolation:** UL 94 V-0 flame-retardant red polyester standoffs provide 1000V dielectric isolation for dedicated isolated ground (IG) circuits in data centers and telecom facilities

**AND THE PART EVERYONE MISSES**
Adiabatic thermal cross-section calculation ($S = \frac{\sqrt{I^2 t}}{k}$).

Under IEC 60364-5-54, sizing a ground busbar to steady-state ampacity is fundamentally wrong. Ground bars carry virtually zero current during normal operations; their dimensions are governed entirely by short-time prospective fault currents. A 25kA / 0.5s fault demands a minimum $100.4\text{ mm}^2$ copper cross-section regardless of the panel's nominal load rating.

## 2 — High Voltage Current Transformer: 10kV–35kV Outdoor Instrument CT Sizing

**HOOK**
Never, under any circumstances, disconnect a secondary circuit on an energized high-voltage current transformer.

With zero secondary current, 100% of the primary transmission current forces the core into violent magnetic saturation, generating lethal peak voltages exceeding 5,000V that will instantly flash over and destroy the terminal box.

**PROBLEM**
Specifying outdoor instrument current transformers in medium-voltage (10kV to 35kV) distribution yards.

Engineers must balance extreme environmental exposure (UV radiation, salt spray, thermal cycling) against strict tariff revenue metering accuracy (Class 0.2S) and heavy fault-current protection linearity (Class 5P20) without core saturation.

**SOLUTION**
Outdoor **LZZBW / YM-10KV Series Vacuum-Cast Cycloaliphatic Epoxy Resin (HCEP) High-Voltage Current Transformers**.

Three medium-voltage engineering pillars:
&bull; **Hydrophobic Cycloaliphatic Epoxy (HCEP):** Monolithic vacuum-cast insulation body with deep aerodynamic creepage sheds ($\geq 31\text{ mm/kV}$), preventing dry-band arcing and tracking even under heavy marine salt fog and industrial pollution
&bull; **Multi-core architecture:** Independent nanocrystalline Class 0.2S revenue metering cores paired with linear cold-rolled grain-oriented steel 5P20 protection cores that maintain accuracy up to 20x nominal fault current
&bull; **Hermetic secondary terminal box:** IP67-sealed cast aluminium enclosure at the base with threaded conduit fittings, protecting secondary terminations against moisture ingress over a 30-year design life

**AND THE PART EVERYONE MISSES**
Secondary burden matching and the advantage of 1A secondaries.

In modern outdoor distribution substations, control houses are often located 100 to 200 meters away from the outdoor switchyard. A standard 5A CT secondary circulating through long copper cable runs creates massive $I^2R$ lead resistance that exceeds the CT's rated VA burden, destroying metering accuracy. Specifying a 1A secondary reduces cable burden losses by a factor of 25 ($5^2 / 1^2 = 25$), enabling ultra-long lead runs with small-gauge wire.

&bull; Exceptional impulse withstand (BIL up to 200kV peak on 35kV class)
&bull; Rated short-time thermal current ($I_{th}$) withstand up to 31.5kA for 1 second
&bull; Manufactured and calibrated strictly to IEC 61869-1/2 and IEEE C57.13 standards
