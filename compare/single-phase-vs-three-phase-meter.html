Single Phase vs Three Phase Energy Meter: Which One Do You Need?

A practical comparison for B2B buyers: cost, wiring complexity, accuracy, communication protocols, and real-world applications. Learn when a three-phase meter is required — and when single phase is enough.

Updated: July 2026By Yomin Electric Engineering Team6 min read

Quick Summary: The Core Difference

Single phase: Two wires (line + neutral), 220-240V, up to ~10kW. Used in residential and small commercial. Three phase: Four wires (3 lines + neutral), 380-415V, 10kW-500kW+. Required for industrial equipment, commercial buildings, and utility metering.

When to Choose Single Phase

Single phase energy meters are the right choice when:

  • Your electrical supply is 220-240V, 2-wire (standard in most residential buildings worldwide)
  • Your total connected load is under 10kW (typical for homes, apartments, small retail shops)
  • You only need to measure active energy (kWh) for billing or monitoring
  • Installation simplicity matters — single-phase meters are plug-and-play DIN rail devices
  • Budget is the primary concern — single-phase meters cost 60-80% less than equivalent three-phase

Real example: A 50-unit apartment building in Nairobi, Kenya. Each unit has a single-phase supply (220V, 40A max). The developer installs 50 × YEM011AG single-phase DIN rail meters with RS485 — central reading via a data concentrator. Total project cost: ~$500 in meters.

When Three Phase Is Required

You need a three-phase energy meter when:

  • Your supply is 3-phase, 380-415V — common in commercial and industrial buildings
  • Connected load exceeds 10kW — three-phase motors, HVAC systems, production machinery
  • You need per-phase monitoring — identifying imbalance, individual phase loads, power factor per phase
  • The application requires revenue-grade accuracy (Class 0.5S) — utility billing, large industrial consumer metering
  • You need bi-directional measurement — solar PV systems with grid export, energy storage systems
  • Remote communication is required — three-phase meters come with built-in 4G, WiFi, or LoRa

Real example: A textile factory in Dhaka, Bangladesh. 150kW total load across 15 three-phase motors and lighting circuits. The main incomer uses a Class 0.5S CT-operated three-phase meter (YEM021GC-N) with RS485 MODBUS feeding into a SCADA energy management system. Cost: ~$70 for the meter + $4.50 each for 3 × 400/5A CTs.

Can You Use a Three-Phase Meter on a Single-Phase Supply?

Technically yes — a three-phase meter will function when connected to a single-phase supply (wiring L1 and Neutral only). The meter will display zero for L2 and L3. However, this is not recommended for permanent installations because:

  • You pay 2-4× more for unused capability
  • The meter displays zero readings for 2/3 of its channels — confusing for end users
  • Some utility regulations prohibit this configuration for billing

Use a single-phase meter on single-phase supply. Use a three-phase meter on three-phase supply.

Key Takeaway

Single phase = residential and small commercial. Simple, affordable, covers 90% of basic metering needs. Three phase = industrial, commercial buildings, utility-grade. Higher cost but essential for loads above 10kW, per-phase monitoring, and revenue metering. If in doubt, check your supply — if you have a 4-pole main breaker, you have three-phase supply and need a three-phase meter.
